Career ranks show your position inside your current airline. Your rank can carry over when you change airline.

Ranks affect pay, promotion path, and long-term progression. They do not replace type ratings. You still need the right Career type rating or active training path for the aircraft you want to fly.

## Rank order

Career Mode uses this rank path:

1. First Officer.
2. Senior First Officer.
3. Captain.
4. Senior Captain.

You start as a First Officer path pilot after onboarding. When you change airline, the new airline hires you at a rank based on your current rank and the two airlines' tiers. See [Rank when you change airline](#rank-when-you-change-airline).

## What each rank does

| Rank | Main benefit |
| --- | --- |
| First Officer | Entry rank. Lets you build employer hours, reputation, seniority, and type-rating history. |
| Senior First Officer | Uses the First Officer pay band with a 12% senior rank multiplier. |
| Captain | Moves into the Captain pay band, which is higher than the First Officer band, and unlocks Historical Operations when the employer has an archive. |
| Senior Captain | Uses the Captain pay band with a 12% senior rank multiplier. |

Ranks mainly improve career value through pay and progression. Dispatch access still depends on employer fleet, type ratings, contract state, medical clearance, line checks, and current location.

## Promotion basics

Promotions are offered by your current airline when you meet the requirements.

FlyHub checks:

- Employer hours with the current airline.
- Continuous tenure with the current airline.
- Reputation.
- Recent performance.
- Current rank.
- Existing promotion offers or pending promotion contracts.

When a promotion is ready, it appears as a promotion offer. You must sign the new promotion contract before the new rank and compensation become active.

## How the promotion ladder works

Smaller airlines promote faster. Bigger airlines take longer and ask for better flying, but pay much more.

- **Employer hours set the pace.** Flying more gets you promoted sooner.
- **Tenure is only a minimum.** Waiting alone never promotes you.
- Hours are sized so a pilot flying about 13 hours a week meets the hours and the weeks at about the same time.

Tenure counts from the start of your current stay with the airline. Training at the airline counts, and promotions keep it running.

## Promotion requirements by tier

The table shows the base requirements before skills, union membership and contract length.

| Promotion | 1-star | 2-star | 3-star | 4-star | 5-star |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Senior First Officer | 25h, 2w, 6.4 rep, 7.2 perf | 35h, 3w, 6.4 rep, 7.3 perf | 40h, 3w, 6.4 rep, 7.4 perf | 50h, 4w, 7.2 rep, 7.5 perf | 55h, 4w, 8.6 rep, 7.6 perf |
| Captain | 65h, 5w, 7.4 rep, 7.8 perf | 80h, 6w, 7.4 rep, 7.9 perf | 90h, 7w, 7.4 rep, 8.0 perf | 115h, 9w, 7.4 rep, 8.1 perf | 130h, 10w, 8.6 rep, 8.2 perf |
| Senior Captain | 120h, 9w, 8.4 rep, 8.4 perf | 145h, 11w, 8.4 rep, 8.5 perf | 170h, 13w, 8.4 rep, 8.6 perf | 210h, 16w, 8.4 rep, 8.7 perf | 240h, 18w, 8.6 rep, 8.8 perf |

In this table:

- `h` means employer hours with the current airline.
- `w` means continuous tenure weeks with the current airline.
- `rep` means Career reputation. It is never lower than the reputation the airline needs to hire you, which is why 4-star and 5-star airlines ask for more.
- `perf` means recent performance score. It rises by 0.1 per star.

Recent performance is based on your recent scored Career flights with the current airline.

**My Company** shows your airline's ladder under **Promotion Ladder**. Your skills, union membership and contract discounts are already applied, and your next rank shows your current tenure, hours, reputation and performance against what it needs.

## What shortens a promotion

These discounts stack with each other. Together they can cut hours or tenure by at most half.

| Source | Hours | Tenure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Union membership | −10% | −10%, plus 0.1 less reputation and recent performance |
| Standard contract (26 weeks) | — | −10% |
| Long-term contract (52 weeks) | — | −20% |
| Accelerated Upgrade (Pilot Skills) | −10% / −20% / −30% | −15% / −30% / −45% |
| Command Upgrade (Pilot Skills) | — | −10% |
| Company Loyalty (Pilot Skills) | −5% | — |
| Company Career (Pilot Skills) | −10% | −10% |

- Skill discounts add together first. Union, contract and skills then combine as separate discounts.
- The contract discount uses the longest contract you signed during your current stay with the airline. A short promotion contract does not take it away.
- Union membership opens after 60 days with an active signed employment contract, and costs monthly dues. See [Career Economy and Airline Tiers](/career-economy-tiers#union-dues).
- Tenure never drops below 1 week for Senior First Officer, 3 weeks for Captain and 6 weeks for Senior Captain.

## Rank when you change airline

Your rank can come with you when you join another airline. It depends on the new airline's tier compared with the airline where you held the rank:

| New airline | Rank you are hired at |
| --- | --- |
| Same tier or lower | Your current rank |
| One tier higher | One rank lower, for example Captain joins as Senior First Officer |
| Two or more tiers higher | First Officer |

- Senior Captain is seniority at one airline, so it carries over as Captain.
- If you are between jobs, FlyHub uses the rank you held at your last airline.
- The **Direct Entry** skill lets an airline one tier higher hire you at your current rank.
- Tenure and seniority still start again at the new airline.
- Job Market offers and airline-sent offers both show the rank you would be hired at.

This gives you two ways to build a career:

- **Climb inside one airline.** Join the biggest airline you qualify for and work up its ladder.
- **Climb the tiers.** Make Captain quickly at a smaller airline, then move up one tier at a time as Senior First Officer or Captain.

## Promotion contracts

A promotion is not active just because you qualify.

When FlyHub creates a promotion offer:

1. Open Job Market.
2. Review the promotion offer.
3. Review the new rank and pay.
4. Sign the promotion contract.
5. Return to Career pages after the contract is active.

Promotion contracts keep you with the same airline. Your employer tenure and seniority remain continuous.

## Promotion pay

Promotion pay is calculated from rank, airline tier, region, and airline pay profile.

Promotion offers also use raise protection. The promotion hourly rate is generated from the airline/rank band, but it must be at least a 10% raise over your current hourly rate.

This means promotion should feel meaningful even if the generated band is close to your current pay.

For pay bands by tier and rank, see [Career Economy and Airline Tiers](/career-economy-tiers#base-pay-by-tier-and-rank).

## Why you may not see a promotion yet

You may not see a promotion if:

- You do not have enough employer hours.
- You do not have enough continuous tenure.
- Your reputation is too low.
- Your recent performance is too low.
- You already have an unread promotion offer.
- You already have a pending promotion contract.
- You are already at Senior Captain.

Check Career Dashboard, My Company, Job Market, and Notifications for clues.

## Rank and type ratings

Rank and type ratings are separate systems.

Rank says what role you hold in the company. Type ratings say which aircraft families you can fly.

For example:

- A Captain without the right type rating cannot fly that family.
- A First Officer with a valid type rating can fly eligible company assignments for that family.
- A pilot in training may only see the family they are training on until training is complete.

See [Career Game Loop](/career-game-loop#type-ratings) for the type-rating guide.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking a promotion happens automatically without signing the promotion contract.
- Expecting rank to unlock aircraft without the right type rating.
- Switching employers right before meeting promotion requirements. Tenure starts again at the new airline.
- Ignoring recent performance. Hours alone are not enough.
- Joining the union without budgeting for monthly dues.
- Comparing two pilots without checking airline tier, region, union status, contract length, and current employer tenure.
- Jumping two tiers up and expecting to keep your rank. Moving one tier at a time keeps more of it.
