# Manage Airline: Programs

**Programs** are operating contracts the airline pays for every day. In return, every flight settled while a contract runs earns more or costs less. Programs are also the only way to raise the airline's reputation ceiling above 7.

The page also shows what is limiting the airline today, how long the cash lasts, and how much standby pricing is saving.

## Open Programs

1. Open an active airline from **Airlines**.
2. Open its profile and select **Manage airline**.
3. Select **Programs**.

Programs follows Finance access. Owners, Managers, and Finance staff normally see it. You need finance permission to contract or end a program.

## Sustainability

The top of the page answers whether the airline can carry its commitments.

| Item | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| What limits the airline today | The one thing holding the airline back right now, with a button to the page that fixes it. |
| Cash lasts | Cash divided by the daily loss over the last 30 days of flying, after flight income and fixed costs. **Covered by flying** means the airline earns at least what it spends. |
| Break-even | Flights a week needed to pay fixed costs per day at your average net per flight, and how many you fly now. |
| Reputation | Current reputation out of 10, its cap, and how much programs add to what passengers see. |
| Program fees | Total program fees per day, and the next contract to end. |

**Cash lasts**, **Break-even**, **Fixed costs per day**, and **Average net per flight** are the same numbers as on [Finance](./manage-finance#shared-numbers-with-programs).

The limit is checked in this order, and the first one that applies is shown:

| Limit | When it appears | Suggested action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash | Cash runs out in less than 30 days at the current pace. | Return idle leases or close routes nobody flies. |
| Activity | No settled flights in the last 30 days. | Get pilots flying again. |
| Pilots | Flights are below break-even and fewer than 3 pilots flew in 30 days. | Recruit pilots. |
| Activity | Flights are below break-even. | Fill the schedules the airline already pays for. |
| Fleet | Aircraft average less than one flight a week each. | Return idle leases. |
| Network | More than half of the schedules are in standby. | Concentrate frequency on fewer routes. |
| Reputation | Reputation is below 4 and no program is running. | Contract catering, crew training, or a campaign. |
| Growth | The operation covers its costs. | Reinvest the margin in programs. |

The same strip appears on the management [Overview](./manage-overview) and [Finance](./manage-finance). **Cash lasts** assumes the airline keeps flying as it has been. Finance's **If nobody flies** is the worst case with no flight income; see [Shared numbers with Programs](./manage-finance#shared-numbers-with-programs).

## Side panel

| Section | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Commitments | Running and scheduled contracts, days left, and program fees per day. Contracts with 14 days or less left show **Renew**. |
| Can the airline carry more? | Cash, **Fixed costs per day**, **Average net per flight**, and, when anything is idle, how much standby pricing saves. A new program adds its daily fee to fixed costs. |
| Empire score | Collapsed by default. See [Empire score](#empire-score). |

## Program branches

The roadmap has six branches. Each level needs the level below it, and higher levels also need real flying behind them.

### Cabin & catering

What passengers feel on board.

| Level | Effect | Extra requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Standard service | +4% cabin revenue, +0.3 perceived reputation | None |
| 2. Signature service | +8% cabin revenue, +0.6 perceived reputation | 25 settled flights in 90 days |
| 3. Premium cabin | +12% cabin revenue, +0.9 perceived reputation | 100 settled flights in 90 days, Crew training level 1 |

### Crew

Training and standards. Fewer passenger compensations.

| Level | Effect | Extra requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Recurrent training | −30% compensations | None |
| 2. Standards department | −50% compensations | 25 settled flights in 90 days |
| 3. Training academy | −70% compensations | 100 settled flights in 90 days, reputation 4 |

### Maintenance

Reliability over time.

| Level | Effect | Extra requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Line maintenance | −8% airframe operating cost, −25% unscheduled repairs | None |
| 2. Reliability program | −14% airframe operating cost, −40% unscheduled repairs | 3 operating aircraft |
| 3. Heavy maintenance partner | −20% airframe operating cost, −50% unscheduled repairs | 6 operating aircraft, 60 settled flights in 90 days |

### Ground operations

Your own handling at network stations.

| Level | Effect | Extra requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hub handling | −6% handling and landing charges | 1 hub |
| 2. Network handling | −12% handling and landing charges | 2 hubs, 40 settled flights in 90 days |

### Commercial

Marketing campaigns that lift how the market sees the airline. Campaigns run for 30 days only.

| Level | Effect | Extra requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Regional campaign | +0.6 perceived reputation | 1 open route |
| 2. National campaign | +1.1 perceived reputation | 25 settled flights in 90 days |
| 3. Brand campaign | +1.5 perceived reputation | 80 settled flights in 90 days, Catering level 1 |

### Fuel & risk

Negotiated supply. Fuel is the biggest cost line.

| Level | Effect | Extra requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Preferred supplier | −4% fuel price | 2 operating aircraft |
| 2. Volume agreement | −8% fuel price | 5 operating aircraft, 40 settled flights in 90 days |

Every catering, crew, maintenance, and ground level also raises the reputation ceiling. See [Reputation ceiling](#reputation-ceiling).

## Roadmap states

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Locked | A requirement is missing. The node shows how many. |
| Available | Ready to contract. The node shows the price per day. |
| Scheduled | Contracted and waiting for its start date. |
| Active | Running. The node shows the days left. |
| Expiring | 14 days or less left. It can be renewed. |
| Included | A lower level covered by a higher active level. |

## Contract a program

1. Select an available level on the roadmap.
2. Review **What it does** and **Requirements**.
3. Choose a contract length of 30, 60, or 90 days. Longer contracts are discounted 4% for 60 days and 8% for 90 days.
4. Review the price per day, the setup fee, the total over the term, and the estimated value per month.
5. Select **Contract and pay**.

To contract, the airline needs the setup fee plus 7 days of the daily fee in cash. If it is short, the button is disabled and the dialog says how much more is needed.

The setup fee is paid when you sign. The daily fee is charged every night at 00:10 UTC with the airline's other fixed costs, even if cash goes below zero. A contract starts after a short lead time of one to four days, shown in the dialog.

The dialog estimates whether the program pays for itself at the airline's current flying. If the fee outweighs the direct return, it says so. Programs that raise the reputation ceiling can still be worth it for the long term.

### Prices

Prices grow with the size of the airline (aircraft in service and pilots who flew in the last 30 days). The roadmap shows the current multiplier, for example **Prices ×1.23 for 4 aircraft and 6 active pilots**.

Prices also move a little each week with supplier prices. The dialog says whether supplier prices are above normal, below normal, or normal this week. Signing when they are below normal locks the lower price for the whole term.

## Renew, upgrade, or end a contract

- **Renew**: opens 14 days before a contract ends. The renewal starts the day after the current contract and has no setup fee.
- **Upgrade**: contracting the next level replaces the running contract after the new level's lead time.
- **End**: asks for confirmation first. The contract stops tonight: its effects and reputation-cap bonus end with today. A contract that has not started yet is cancelled and never starts. The setup fee and fees already paid are not refunded.

When a contract lapses, its effects stop the same day. The airline keeps the level for 14 days, so it can still contract the next level or renew without starting the ladder again.

## Effects

Effects apply to every flight settled while the contract runs. From one branch only the highest active level counts. Effects from different branches stack.

- **Cabin revenue** raises passenger revenue on each flight.
- **Compensations, repairs, airframe, handling, landing, and fuel** effects lower those costs in the flight settlement.
- **Passengers see +N reputation** adds N to the reputation passengers use for demand and fares. It does not change the airline's real reputation score.

## Reputation ceiling

Reputation runs from 0 to 10, and every airline starts at 1.0. Once a week it moves toward a target built from:

| Part | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Flight scores | 45% |
| Airline age | 20% |
| 90-day profit | 20% |
| Activity | 15% |

It rises at most 0.10 a week (slower above 3) and can drop up to 0.30 a week. Higher reputation means more passengers and better fares.

In short: fly clean, well-scored flights every week to raise reputation, and run catering, crew, maintenance, or ground programs to raise the cap.

Airline reputation can rise only up to its ceiling (the cap). The base ceiling is **7 out of 10**.

Catering, crew training, maintenance, and ground handling levels raise the ceiling while they are active, up to 10. Reaching the top of the reputation scale needs both a strong operating record and investment in programs.

## Standby pricing

Schedules, stations, and leased aircraft idle for 14 days or more are charged a fraction of their daily cost until the next flight. Nothing needs to be configured, and they can still be flown.

| Commitment | Standby charge |
| --- | --- |
| Active schedule | 25% of its daily fee |
| Airport or hub (not the home base) | 25% of its daily fee |
| Leased aircraft | 40% of its daily lease |

The home airport never drops to the standby rate. When anything is idle, the side panel shows how much standby saves per day and how many aircraft, stations, and schedules are at the idle rate. One flight restores the full price.

## Empire score

**Empire score** is a score in points for what the airline has built. It is not cash and cannot be spent, and it does not affect programs. Open it from the bottom of the side panel. It adds up:

| Part | How it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Fleet in service | Daily lease × 60 per aircraft, × 20 for aircraft at the idle rate. |
| Network | Opening fees paid for airport stations, hubs, and open routes. |
| Reputation and pilots | Reputation × 20,000 × pilots who flew in the last 30 days. |
| Programs | Daily fee × 30 for each running program. |

Use it to follow long-term progress when cash is being reinvested.

## In Finance

Program fees appear in [Finance](./manage-finance) as **Program fees** under **Fixed costs per day**, and in the ledger as **Program fees** and **Program setup fees**.
