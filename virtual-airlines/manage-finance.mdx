# Manage Airline: Finance

**Finance** is a read-only view of your airline's cash position, flight results, recurring commitments, forecasts, and transaction history. It does not provide controls for manual payments, adjustments, or settlement edits.

## Open Finance

1. Open an active airline from **Airlines**.
2. Open its profile and select **Manage airline**.
3. Select **Finance**.

Finance is available to active airline or group members with finance access. Owners, Managers, and Finance staff normally have access. Operations, Fleet, Schedules, Pilots, Network, Dispatcher, and pilot-only roles do not receive finance access by default.

## Financial overview

The header shows **Available cash**, **Revenue**, **Costs**, and **Net** for the selected period. Values are shown in FH$. Choose a specific period or **All periods**; the combined view includes up to 12 periods.

The overview can also show:

- financial periods and their revenue, costs, and net result;
- the most recent flight-finance results;
- daily fixed costs for leases, airports, active schedules, and operating programs;
- maintenance already committed;
- the reserve target (30 days of costs plus maintenance) and cash after reserve;
- **If nobody flies**: how many days the cash lasts with no flight income;
- **Flights to break even**: flights per week needed to cover fixed costs;
- 30-, 60-, and 90-day cash projections;
- active leases, airports, and schedules.

Projections assume the last 30 days of flying and today's fixed costs stay the same. They are planning estimates, not guaranteed income.

### Two runway numbers

| Figure | Where | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **If nobody flies** | Finance | Cash minus booked maintenance, divided by the full daily fixed costs. No flight income is counted. |
| **Cash lasts at recent pace** | [Overview](./manage-overview) and [Programs](./manage-programs) | Cash divided by the airline's daily result over the last 30 days, flying included. **Covered by flying** means flights earn more than the airline spends each day. |

The first is the worst case; the second is what happens if the airline keeps flying as it has been.

### Daily fixed costs

Fixed costs are aircraft leases, airport stations and hubs, scheduled routes, and operating programs. There are no staff costs. Finance lists them at full price, before standby discounts.

They are charged from airline cash every night at **00:10 UTC**, even when the balance is already below zero. A schedule is only charged on its UTC operating days. When the next charge is pending, the page shows an estimate of what will be taken at 00:10 UTC.

Anything idle for 14 days or more bills less until it flies again: schedules and airport stations pay 25% (never the home airport), and leased aircraft pay 40%. They can still be flown, and one flight restores the full price. See [Standby pricing](./manage-programs#standby-pricing).

## Settlements

The **Settlements** view lists completed flight finances. It can show the airline, flight, settlement date, revenue, operating costs, crew payroll, net result, and a **View breakdown** link.

Select **View breakdown** to open the flight's Economy record in the VA logbook. **Net** is the final flight result. Do not add crew payroll to the operating cost a second time when interpreting the total.

The list uses 25 records per page. If a completed flight has no available report, the page explains that the report is unavailable.

## Ledger

The **Ledger** is the transaction history. Search by flight or account, filter credits and debits, and filter by period or operating airline when the airline belongs to a group.

Positive movements are credits; negative movements are debits. Flight movements can be expanded to show their accounting details. Recurring lease, airport, schedule, and program charges are grouped by UTC day, while route openings are grouped by UTC week.

The ledger uses 25 rows per page. An expanded movement shows up to 100 detail entries. Use the page controls to move through older results.

## Groups and dates

In a group, cash is shared while Settlements and Ledger keep an **Airline** column so each result remains attributed to its operating airline. Current commitments use open operating airlines; historical results can include retired airlines.

Financial periods are UTC calendar months, and week boundaries also use UTC. A newly completed flight or charge can take a short time to appear in the Finance overview.

## Empty states and recovery

- **No financial periods** means the airline has not created a financial period from completed flights or recurring charges yet.
- **No settled flights** means no completed flight result is available for the selected view.
- **No transactions match** means the period, movement filter, or search text returned no rows.
- If Finance fails to load, use **Try again** or **Refresh finance**.

If the page still fails, reload the management workspace and follow the recovery instruction shown by the page.

## Read Finance with the economy model

Finance answers where the airline's money went; it does not decide whether a route is attractive for you. Compare flight net with recurring leases, airport and hub fees, active schedule fees, program fees, route openings, and the reserve target. A profitable flight can still sit inside an airline that is losing cash overall when its fixed commitments are too large.

Read [Virtual Airlines Economy and Ticket Pricing](./economy-and-pricing) before interpreting a settlement and [From Dispatch to Settlement](./flight-lifecycle) to understand why the final recognized payload and score can differ from the first Dispatch quote.
