# Manage Airline: Network

Use **Airline management → Network** to create and manage route schedules. This is different from the public profile's **Network** tab, which is view-only.

## Open Network

1. Open an active airline from **Airlines**.
2. Open its profile and select **Manage airline**.
3. Select **Network**.

In a group, choose an **Operating airline** when the selector is available. Schedules and fleet belong to the selected operating airline; airports and funds can be shared by the group.

Network access is available to Owners, Managers, Operations, Schedules Managers, and Network Planners according to their assigned access. Creating or editing schedules requires schedule-management access. Opening a new route also requires route and financial access.

## Network summary and schedule list

The summary strip shows each figure with its period underneath:

| Figure | Period | What it counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Airline cash** | Right now | The cash shared by your airline group. |
| **Flying** | Right now | Schedules that publish flights, out of all schedules including paused and retired. |
| **Flights flown** | All time | Settled flights on schedules still listed on this page. |
| **Revenue** | All time | Ticket and cargo income from those flights. |
| **Flight profit** | All time | Settled flight results, before the schedule fee, airport fees, and route opening fees. |

Deleted schedules drop out of these totals; retired schedules stay in them.

Each schedule row shows:

- flight number and status (**Flying**, **Paused**, or **Retired**);
- route, with passenger or cargo, distance, and estimated duration underneath;
- operating days (UTC);
- eligible aircraft;
- flights flown and revenue, both all time.

Search by flight number, airport, or aircraft type. The status filter shows **Flying and paused** by default, or only **Flying**, **Paused**, **Retired**, or **All schedules**. Sort by flights or revenue and use the page controls to browse more results. When nothing matches, the page says whether the search or filter is the cause and offers **Clear search and filter**. If the network has fewer than two airports, it points you to Airports first.

Select a schedule to open its details: its status and what to do next, operating days, duration, the fee per flying day, all-time flights and profit, aircraft estimates, and any upcoming flights (waiting for a pilot, or booked and flying).

## Create or edit a schedule

1. Select **New schedule** or choose **Edit** on an existing schedule.
2. Select different origin and destination airports that are active in the group's network.
3. Choose passenger or cargo.
4. Set the flight-number suffix and operating days. Days are UTC days.
5. Choose aircraft types, categories, sizes, or dynamic inclusions and exclusions.
6. Review the duration and cost estimate.
7. Save the schedule.

Two schedules can share a flight number when they fly different routes, for example an outbound and a return leg. The same flight number on the same route is only allowed once.

Before saving, FlyHub checks the airports, days, aircraft eligibility, airline state, number format, distance, and duration. The saved duration is recalculated from the current operating rules. Schedules with open flights cannot be edited until those flights are resolved.

## Aircraft eligibility

Passenger aircraft can serve passenger schedules, cargo aircraft can serve cargo schedules, and Combi aircraft can support both. A type marked **Other** is not usable until it has a valid classification.

You can pick exact aircraft types, or a whole size or category. With a size or category, aircraft of that kind you lease later join the schedule automatically. If no active aircraft are eligible, add or configure an eligible aircraft in [Fleet](./manage-fleet) before saving.

## Route cost and lifecycle

An existing origin–destination–operation combination can be reused. A suspended route may be reopened when you have route access. Opening a new route requires sufficient group funds and the required route and finance access; FlyHub charges the opening fee from airline cash when it creates the route. The fee is one-time and based on distance, up to FH$30,000. If cash is short, the save button stays disabled and the page says how much more is needed.

Each flying schedule also has a fee per flying day, charged at 00:10 UTC only on its UTC operating days, even if cash is below zero. If the route has no completed flight for 14 days, the fee drops to 25% until someone flies it again.

Schedules are **Flying**, **Paused**, or **Retired**.

- **Pause** stops new flights and cancels upcoming flights that no pilot has booked. Booked flights still fly.
- **Enable** makes a paused schedule fly again when the airline, route, airports, and aircraft are still valid.
- **Retire** stops the schedule for good and moves it to the Retired list, where its flights and earnings stay on record. It can't be undone.
- **Delete** removes the schedule and its totals from the Network page for good. Pilots' logbooks keep the flights they already flew. Use it for schedules created by mistake; retire a schedule that has flown if you want to keep its record here.

Retire and Delete are both unavailable while a pilot has a booked or in-progress flight on the schedule. Wait until those flights finish or are cancelled. Upcoming flights that no pilot has booked are cancelled by either action. If you delete the last schedule on a route, the route is paused and stops charging.

## Import and export

**Export** downloads a CSV of flying and paused schedules, including size or category picks and duration information.

**Import** accepts FlyHub and Newsky CSV files. The page validates the complete file and shows a preview before you confirm. One flight number can fly several routes; identical repeated rows are skipped and counted in the preview. The same flight number and route with different details is reported so you can keep one. The import replaces the selected operating airline's current schedules, preserves existing reservations and completed history, and removes future unclaimed inventory from schedules being replaced. It does not change other operating airlines.

The import dialog validates the file before applying it. Follow the size and schedule limits shown in the dialog, and review the preview before confirming the replacement.

## Limits and recovery

The schedule list starts with 25 rows per page and supports larger page sizes within the page's controls. Both airports must be active in the shared network. A schedule must have at least one operating day and one eligible aircraft.

If Network is empty, use **New schedule** after configuring Airports and Fleet. If a schedule cannot be saved, check the airport pair, operating days, aircraft eligibility, permissions, available balance, and open flights. If a load or update fails, refresh the page and reopen the section. Imports, retirement, and deletion do not have an undo action.

The public profile Network map cannot create or edit schedules. Use this management page for those actions.
