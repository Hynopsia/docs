# Manage Airline: Operations

Use **Operations** to supervise flights operated by your airline or airline group. It brings current activity, upcoming work, closed operations, low-scoring results, and operational trends into one workspace.

Operations is a review and monitoring tool. It is not where you reserve a flight, build an OFP, start tracking, recover another pilot's flight, change a route, or settle a flight.

## Open Operations

1. Open an active airline from **Airlines**.
2. Open its profile and select **Manage airline**.
3. Select **Operations**.

You need Operations access to view the workspace. Reviewing a low-scoring result requires the additional flight-management access assigned by your airline. The exact pages and actions available to you depend on your role.

## Choose a view

Operations provides five views:

- **Operations dashboard** — See the current workload, flight types, and flight scores.
- **Needs attention** — Find flights scored under 7.0 in the last 30 days that have not been reviewed. This list is separate from your airline's strike rule.
- **Active now** — See flights that are flying, interrupted, or resuming.
- **Upcoming** — See flights that are published or booked but haven't started.
- **Completed** — See flights that have finished one way or another: paid, rejected, cancelled, abandoned, or expired.

The summary at the top shows how many flights need attention, are active now, are upcoming, and are completed. Some categories can overlap. For example, a completed flight below 7.0 can remain in **Needs attention** until a manager marks it as reviewed.

## Filter and find operations

Use the operating-airline selector when it is available to view the whole group or one operating airline. Search by flight number or airport, then submit the search. Use the page controls to browse additional results.

The group view keeps the operating airline visible so you can tell which airline performed each flight. Closed operating airlines may remain in historical results even though they no longer accept new operations.

## Read an operation

Select a row to open its details. Depending on the flight, the detail panel can include:

- flight number and route;
- planned departure and flight source;
- operating status and tracking status;
- aircraft registration, type, and current position when available;
- pilot and reservation;
- score and result status;
- pay status, and the month whose results the flight counts toward;
- the outcome, such as Completed as planned, Arrived late, Diverted, or Emergency landing;
- the actual destination;
- for a rejected flight, why it was rejected and what that cost (see below);
- recent operational events;
- buttons to open the pilot profile and the flight's logbook report.

The detail view is for inspection. It does not give a manager control of another pilot's active flight.

## Review a low-scoring result

When a result below 7.0 appears in **Needs attention**, open it and select **Mark as reviewed** if that action is available to you.

Marking a result as reviewed:

- records that the team checked it;
- removes it from the unreviewed attention count when appropriate;
- does not change the result, score, settlement, or logbook entry;
- does not reopen, validate, correct, or recover the flight;
- does not remove strikes. Strikes are counted separately in [People](./manage-people).

Flight validation happens during flight completion. Operations only records the administrative review after the result exists.

## Insights and history

The **Operations dashboard** has three charts:

- **Current workload** counts every flight by where it is now: needs attention, active, upcoming, or closed.
- **Flight types** counts flights planned to depart in the last 30 days, by where they came from (schedule, charter, free flight, and so on).
- **Flight scores** counts flights checked in the last 30 days in the bands 9.0–10.0, 8.0–8.9, 7.0–7.9, below 7.0, no score, and voided for a rule breach.

Flight types and scores always cover the same 30 days, whatever page of flights you are looking at. If an airline has more than 5,000 flights in that period, the dashboard says so and counts the first 5,000.

The operational history panel can cover:

- the last 30 days;
- the last 90 days;
- the last year;
- the full available period.

The chart helps you spot trends; it is separate from the individual flight detail and does not replace the financial, network, or management-history pages.

## Statuses and recovery

Statuses use plain labels with a short hint underneath:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Flying | In progress. |
| Interrupted | The sim or app stopped mid-flight. The aircraft is held until the pilot resumes or the flight is closed. |
| Resuming | The pilot is restoring the saved position. |
| Checking | Waiting for the flight report to be checked. |
| Paid | Accepted and settled. |
| Rejected | Failed validation. No pay. |
| Cancelled | No pay and no cost. |
| Abandoned | Closed before it finished. No pay. |
| Expired | Never started in time. |

Flight execution and recovery belong to the assigned pilot in **My Flights** and **Active Flight**. Managers cannot resume or recover another pilot's flight from Operations. When a recovery deadline applies to an interrupted flight, the hint shows until when the pilot can resume; after that the flight is closed and the aircraft released. A closed attempt does not create a new flight record or settlement.

### Rejected flights

A rejected flight shows **Why it was rejected** once, in plain words, followed by what the rejection cost:

- **Crash** — no pay or XP. The detail shows the amount the airline was charged, how many condition points every aircraft part lost, and when the aircraft's grounding ends. These numbers are read from the saved result, so they always match what was actually applied (currently FH$1,000,000, 10 points, and 24 hours).
- **Rule breach** — the recorder caught time acceleration that the airline's rules don't allow, or an aircraft movement a real flight can't make (for example slewing or teleporting). The flight is voided: no score, pay, XP, or wear, and the aircraft stays where it was before the flight. Lists show these flights as **Rule breach** or **Voided** instead of a score.
- **Other rejections** — for example the wrong route, or an offline report when the airline only accepts live tracking. No pay or XP. The aircraft is recorded where it actually landed, when that is known.

## Limits and timing

- The workspace normally shows 25 operations per page.
- Searches and page sizes are limited by the current workspace controls.
- A flight detail shows a limited set of recent operational events.
- The low-score review queue covers recent results, normally the last 30 days.
- Newly completed or reviewed activity can take a short time to appear.

The page's current controls are the final authority if these limits change.

## Loading, empty, and recovery states

Operations can show separate loading states for permission checks, the operation list, and historical data. Each list has its own empty message that says what would appear there; for example **Nobody is flying right now** on Active now, or a note to start a schedule or offer a charter on Upcoming. A search with no matches says so and suggests checking the flight number or airport code.

If the list cannot load, use **Retry** or reload Operations. If a review action is unavailable, confirm that the result is below 7.0, is still eligible for review, and that your role includes the required access. Follow the recovery instruction shown by the page.

## How Operations differs from other pages

- **Dispatch** finds, quotes, and reserves flights.
- **My Flights / Active Flight** lets the assigned pilot prepare, track, complete, abort, or recover a flight.
- **Calendar** organizes your own scheduled and reserved flights.
- **Network** manages airports, routes, schedules, and aircraft eligibility.
- **Finance** shows cash, costs, settlements, and commitments.
- **Market audit** shows demand, route health, and reputation.
- **Management history** records administrative changes and their reasons.

Use Operations to understand what happened operationally and whether a result needs review; use the other pages for the action that follows.
