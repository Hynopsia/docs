# Manage Airline: Market Audit

The management workspace separates **Network** from **Market audit**. Network manages route schedules; Market audit helps you understand passenger and cargo demand, route health, airport pressure, and reputation. Market audit is a read-only analysis view.

## Open Market audit

1. Open an active airline from **Airlines**.
2. Open its profile and select **Manage airline**.
3. Select **Market audit**.

Market audit is available to active airline members with network-management visibility. Financial contribution figures are shown only when your role also has finance access.

## What the audit shows

The overview can show:

- active routes compared with the total;
- routes with weekly numbers;
- passenger demand and cargo demand;
- the share of demand already booked by all airlines;
- average load factor (how full aircraft flew, against a 65% target);
- route profit when finance figures are available;
- reputation and its change from the previous period.

### Routes

Each route row shows its **Load factor**, **Pool share**, **Profit**, and **Status** for the latest week.

- **Load factor** is how full the aircraft flew (payload ÷ capacity) on completed flights. Cancelled and expired bookings do not count. The bar marks the 65% target.
- **Pool share** is how much of the origin airport's weekly pool in that direction your route carried, for example 1.3K of SKBO's southwest pool of 6.3K (21%). Every airline flying that direction shares the same pool.
- **Profit** is the result of the route's flights for the week, before daily schedule, airport, and route fees.

Select a route to see its status, the reason in plain numbers, a suggested next step, and a week-by-week table. Weeks with no completed flights show as **Not flown** instead of 0%.

| Status | Meaning | Suggested step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Losing money** | Below zero for three flown weeks in a row. | Check aircraft cost, schedule, and price before cutting the route. |
| **Lost money this week** | One negative week. Not enough to suspend the route. | Wait for next week's result. |
| **No flights** | Nothing was flown on the route in the latest week. | Fly it, or move its aircraft to a busier route. |
| **Plane too big** | The last two flown weeks were below 65% full. | Try a smaller aircraft or fewer weekly flights. |
| **Room to grow** | Profitable, 90%+ full, and demand is still unserved. | Add a flight or use a bigger aircraft. |
| **On track** | Nothing in the numbers calls for a change. | None. |
| **Not flown yet** | The route has no weekly numbers. | Complete a flight on it. |

A status is guidance only; it does not edit a route, change a schedule, or reserve demand automatically.

### Airport demand

Airport demand pools are weekly (ISO week) and shared by every airline; each booking takes from them. Demand is shown separately for passengers and cargo, for each of eight directions: north, northeast, east, southeast, south, southwest, west, and northwest.

Each pool opens through the week: 3/7 on Monday, a day more each day, and all of it from Friday. Unused demand stays open. The page shows, for each airport and direction:

- **Weekly pool**: the full week's demand;
- **Taken**: what all airlines have booked this week;
- **Open now**: what can still be booked today.

See [Daily release](./economy-and-pricing#daily-release) for an example.

Use the airport search to find an airport by code, name, or city. The page can show a radial chart and a directional table for the selected airport.

### Reputation

The reputation view can show overall reputation out of 10, recent periods, and change over time. Reputation moves weekly toward a target built from flight scores (45%), airline age (20%), 90-day profit margin (20%), and activity (15%); higher reputation means better fares. See [Reputation ceiling](./manage-programs#reputation-ceiling).

Reliability, operations, passenger experience, and community strength are side indicators on a 0–100 scale. They do not change the reputation score. If no reputation history exists, the view explains that no history is available yet.

## Filters and actions

Use the current view controls to:

- refresh the analysis;
- page through routes and airports;
- sort route rows by load factor, pool share, or profit;
- filter for **Needs a look**, **Room to grow**, **On track**, or **Not flown yet**;
- search airports;
- open a route or airport detail.

Market audit does not open or close routes, set prices, reserve demand, or suspend flights. Use [Network](./manage-network) for schedule changes and [Finance](./manage-finance) for the financial ledger.

## Weekly market and groups

The audit uses the currently published weekly market. A future prepared week is not the same as today's available demand. In a group, demand and network context can be shared while route schedules and operating results remain associated with the selected operating airline.

The available demand can change when other operations commit it, or when aircraft capacity, payload limits, route conditions, reputation, or aircraft condition affect an operation. Use the demand and route indicators together; no single number explains every route.

## Roles and limits

Owner, Manager, Operations, Schedules Manager, and Network Planner access can vary by assigned permissions. Finance staff can see financial information in Finance without automatically receiving Network access. A pilot-only membership cannot open the management audit.

The page uses paginated route and airport lists. It can display recent health and reputation periods but does not promise an unlimited history. The current data source has a fixed weekly snapshot; do not treat the view as live traffic.

## Loading and recovery

During loading, the page uses a skeleton. A failed initial load offers **Try again**; a refresh failure can keep the previous results visible while the new request is retried. Empty states can mean no matching routes, no health data, no airport matches, no reputation history, or no current weekly market.

If Market audit cannot load, select **Try again** or reload the management page. Analysis recommendations are not commands, and missing data is not proof that the route has been deleted.

## Read the market numbers

Demand is finite and directional. A high-demand airport can still have a weak route if the airline's price, frequency, aircraft size, condition, or reputation does not fit the available customers. Use the audit to find the reason, then make changes in Network, Fleet, Airports, or the operating policy.
