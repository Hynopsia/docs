# Career Fatigue

Career fatigue discourages unrealistic grinding without blocking normal flying.

Fatigue only affects Career scoring. It does not reduce payroll. Payroll stays based on real tracked flight time.

## What the dashboard shows

The Career Dashboard shows your Pilot Condition:

| Condition | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Fit for Duty | No fatigue scoring deduction is active. |
| Elevated Fatigue | Fatigue pressure is rising, but no deduction is active yet. |
| Fatigued | Career scoring is currently receiving a fatigue deduction. |
| Severely Fatigued | The maximum fatigue deduction is active until you rest. |

The dashboard also shows:

- Fatigue pressure: your current fatigue score.
- 24h duty: recent tracked time and scheduled duty in the last 24 hours.
- Recent flights: Career flights completed inside the 48-hour activity window.
- Rest status: when the current pressure is expected to recover.
- Main reason: the biggest factor currently affecting fatigue, when one exists.

## What increases fatigue

Fatigue builds when you complete Career flights.

Each completed Career flight adds pressure from:

- The flight itself.
- Real tracked flight time.
- High sim-rate compression.
- Short turnaround time between Career flights.
- Heavy Career duty in the last 24 hours.
- Very high Career flight count in the last 48 hours.

This means fatigue is not only a flight counter. A busy short-haul day, one normal long-haul, and several compressed long-hauls are treated differently.

## Real tracked time

Real tracked time is the real-world time FlyHub tracked for the flight.

Examples:

| Flight | Real tracked time used by fatigue |
| --- | --- |
| 1-hour flight at normal speed | 1 hour |
| 18-hour flight flown at 6x sim rate | About 3 hours |

This keeps fatigue aligned with payroll. If you get paid for 3 real tracked hours, the base fatigue also starts from 3 real tracked hours.

## Sim-rate pressure

Sim rate does not directly reduce salary.

It can increase fatigue pressure because a large Career duty is being compressed into a short real-world session.

Examples:

| Flight | Fatigue effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1x sim rate | No sim-rate pressure. |
| 2x sim rate | Small extra pressure. |
| 6x sim rate | Meaningful extra pressure. |

This prevents sim rate from becoming a free way to stack endless long-haul Career duty in one sitting.

## Short turnarounds

Starting another Career flight soon after the previous one can add fatigue.

| Rest between Career flights | Extra fatigue pressure |
| --- | --- |
| Less than 1 hour | High |
| 1 to 2 hours | Medium |
| 2 to 4 hours | Low |
| 4 hours or more | None |

This is why back-to-back grinding can increase pressure even if each individual flight is short.

## Heavy 24-hour duty

FlyHub also looks at Career duty in the last 24 hours.

Real tracked time matters. Simulated scheduled duty can also matter when sim rate compresses large trips.

Examples:

| Last 24 hours | Fatigue effect |
| --- | --- |
| 6 real tracked hours | Usually normal. |
| More than 8 real tracked hours | Extra fatigue pressure can start. |
| More than 12 real tracked hours | More pressure. |
| More than 24 simulated duty hours | Extra pressure can start. |
| More than 36 simulated duty hours | More pressure. |

## 48-hour activity threshold

The old anti-grind guard still exists as a fallback.

If you complete more than 20 Career flights inside 48 hours, FlyHub can force at least a 3-point fatigue deduction.

This catches extreme short-flight grinding even if the normal fatigue score has not fully caught up yet.

## How fatigue recovers

Fatigue recovers with real-world rest after your last completed Career flight.

Recovery rate:

| Rest time away from Career flying | Fatigue recovery |
| --- | --- |
| 6 hours | About 3 fatigue points |
| 12 hours | About 6 fatigue points |
| 24 hours | About 12 fatigue points |
| 48 hours | About 24 fatigue points |
| 72 hours | Fully reset |

If you do not complete any Career flights for 72 hours, fatigue resets to zero.

## Penalty levels

The maximum fatigue deduction is 4 points.

| Fatigue pressure | Career scoring effect |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 10 | No deduction |
| 10 to 16 | Warning only |
| 16 to 22 | 1-point deduction |
| 22 to 30 | 2-point deduction |
| 30 to 40 | 3-point deduction |
| 40 or more | 4-point deduction |

The 48-hour activity threshold can force at least a 3-point deduction when it applies, but the total fatigue deduction is still capped at 4 points.

## Examples

### One normal short flight

A 1-hour flight at normal speed adds low fatigue pressure.

Result: usually still Fit for Duty.

### Six short flights in one day

Six 1-hour flights at normal speed can be a busy regional day.

Result: usually no scoring deduction, but fatigue pressure may be visible.

### One normal long-haul

An 18-hour long-haul flown at normal speed creates fatigue pressure because it was a long real-world duty.

Result: usually no immediate deduction by itself, but the dashboard may show elevated pressure.

### One compressed long-haul

An 18-hour long-haul flown at 6x sim rate may only count as about 3 real tracked hours for payroll.

Fatigue still adds extra sim-rate pressure because the Career duty was compressed.

Result: usually no immediate deduction by itself, but it is not free fatigue-wise.

### Several compressed long-hauls

If you stack multiple long-hauls at high sim rate in one real-world day, fatigue can move from warning into active deductions.

Result: the first few may be fine, but continued stacking can move you into 1, 2, 3, and eventually 4 points of fatigue deduction.

## What fatigue affects

Fatigue can reduce Career scoring.

That can indirectly affect:

- Flight score.
- Training results.
- Line checks.
- Weekly performance.
- Reputation changes.
- Promotion readiness.
- Airline evaluations.

Fatigue does not directly reduce salary.

## How to avoid fatigue penalties

- Rest between intensive Career sessions.
- Avoid very short turnarounds when already under pressure.
- Watch the 24h duty and 48h flight counts on the dashboard.
- Be careful stacking high sim-rate long-hauls.
- Do not schedule line checks when the dashboard shows active fatigue pressure.
