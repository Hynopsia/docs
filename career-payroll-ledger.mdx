Career payroll and the finance ledger explain where your money came from and where it went.

Use this page when your balance changes and you want to understand why.

## Payroll timing

Career payroll is due every Monday at 13:00 **on that save's Career clock**. After a save has been paused, this can fall on a different day or time in the real world.

- Switching to another save pauses the time remaining until payday.
- Returning to the save resumes that countdown. It does not restart the week or erase the pay you have earned.
- Your active save continues advancing while FlyHub is closed.
- Flying on a real-world Monday does not trigger a payment by itself.

For example, if Save A has **2 hours left until payday**, then you play Save B for three days, Save A still has **2 hours left** when you return. Keep Save A active for those two hours; you do not need to keep the app open.

Payments are processed periodically after they become due, so the deposit may not appear at the exact moment the countdown ends. Check your Finance ledger and payroll summary for the deposit. If payment remains missing after it is due, contact support with the pilot name, save slot, and a screenshot of your payroll details.

See [Career Saves](/career-saves) for more about active and paused saves.

Payroll is not paid after every flight. Flights, bonuses, expenses, and deductions are processed through Career finance rules.

## What payroll can include

Payroll can include:

- Eligible Career flight hours, with at least 1 hour paid per leg.
- The weekly guarantee: at least 4 paid hours in any week you fly.
- Contract hourly rate.
- Performance pay from your weekly reviews.
- Rank pay.
- Airline tier and the airline's pay market.
- Training pay multiplier.
- Reserve pay.
- Bid Board bonus.
- On-time completion bonus.
- Seniority bonus.
- Tax or social contribution deductions.
- Union dues.
- Loan payments.
- Loan interest.
- Late fees.
- Property income.
- Rent or housing support.
- Daily living costs.
- Medical checks.
- Personal events.

For the economy model behind pay and costs, see [Career Economy and Airline Tiers](/career-economy-tiers).

## How your rate is built

The **Next paycheck** panel in Finance shows the formula for your own contract:

```text
tier base × rank × pay market = contract rate
contract rate × (1 + seniority + union) × performance = what you earn per hour
```

Under it, FlyHub lists the next ways your rate goes up: when your next seniority step lands, how a strong week moves performance pay, and what your next promotion pays. If minimum credit or the weekly guarantee added paid hours this week, the panel says so.

See [How your pay is calculated](/career-economy-tiers#how-your-pay-is-calculated) for every number in the formula.

## Main pay modifiers

| Modifier | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Rank | Higher ranks pay more than lower ranks. |
| Airline tier | Higher-tier airlines generally pay better. |
| Pay market | The airline's home country sets the market (0.68x to 1.15x). |
| Tax-free base | No income tax when you are based in a tax-free country such as the UAE or Qatar. |
| Training status | Some training contracts pay a reduced rate. |
| Reserve | Accepted reserve calls pay 2.0x hourly pay. |
| Bid Board | Eligible awarded bid-board flying can add a 15% bonus. |
| On-time completion | Eligible on-time work can add an additional bonus. |
| Seniority | +1% every 8 weeks at your airline, up to 10%. |
| Union | Members add +1% every 2 months, up to 5%. |
| Performance | Weekly reviews move it between 92% and 110%. It follows you to every airline. |
| Minimum credit | Every leg pays at least 1 hour. |
| Weekly guarantee | A week with at least one leg pays at least 4 hours. |

## Grouped weekly living costs

The Account ledger can combine recurring living entries into a row such as **Weekly living costs · 7 items**. This keeps rent, groceries, meals, transport, utilities, subscriptions, gifts, and similar small costs from filling the page with repetitive rows.

Select the group to open its receipt-style breakdown. The detail view shows the original categories, amounts, dates, and running balances.

Grouping is only a display choice. It does not merge transactions or change your balance. Salary, taxes, loan payments, medical charges, union dues, property purchases, and other non-living activity remain separate.

The group represents the ledger's displayed week. If you search for an item inside a group, FlyHub keeps the matching weekly bundle together so the total remains understandable.

## Payroll summary

The payroll summary can explain the larger weekly result.

Use it to see:

- Paid hours.
- Effective hourly rate.
- Bonuses.
- Deductions.
- Loan payments.
- Living expenses.
- Medical status.
- Credit score changes.

If the ledger shows many entries on the same day, the payroll summary is usually the easiest place to understand the full week.

## Protected take-home

FlyHub caps normal mandatory deductions at 25% of gross weekly payroll. Tax, housing, normal living costs, and normal commuting share this combined limit.

The ledger can still show each category separately. When the uncapped total is too high, FlyHub reduces the applied entries so their combined charge stays within the limit.

Loans, voluntary union dues, penalties, property purchases, medical charges, and personal events are processed separately and can reduce take-home below 75%.

## Away-from-base ledger entries

Away-from-base time from company flying is airline-covered. It should not appear as a lodging penalty or hidden overnight charge.

A commuting cost entry can include a small personal away-from-base overstay cost only when you stay away from base for more than 48 hours without accepted company work or an active/upcoming Career booking from that airport.

You may still see commuting cost entries for normal weekly commuting, local transport, or explicit repositioning such as a jumpseat ticket. If a repositioning action is airline-sponsored, the app can show it as company-covered and avoid charging your Career balance.

Older ledger history may still show previous away-from-base wording, but normal company layovers should not create hotel or overnight stay charges.

For the full explanation, see [Career Finance](/career-finance#away-from-base-expenses).

## Loans and credit

Loans affect future payroll because payments are recurring.

Missed loan payments can:

- Add late fees.
- Reduce credit score.
- Reduce reputation.
- Lead to loan default after repeated missed payments.

Do not judge affordability only by current balance. Check the weekly payment pressure in Finance.

## Property income

Property income belongs only to Career Mode.

Property can help long-term cash flow, but the upfront cost can make your short-term balance risky. Buy property only when you can still cover medical checks, loans, relocation, training, and normal living costs.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting payroll immediately after each flight.
- Reading Account history as a future forecast.
- Missing loan pressure because the current balance looks healthy.
- Forgetting that bonuses can appear only when the related flying is processed.
- Ignoring small recurring living costs.
- Confusing Free Flight XP with Career payroll.
