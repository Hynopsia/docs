# Career Base and Current Location

Career Mode tracks both your base and your current location.

They are not always the same thing.

## Base

Your base is the airport your airline assigns you to.

Base affects:

- Career routing.
- Dispatch options.
- Company planning.
- Relocation decisions.
- Some cost and commuting logic.

You normally choose a base during onboarding or a job offer. You can also move base from My Company when the app allows it.

## Current location

Your current location is where your pilot is right now.

It can change after:

- Completing a Career flight.
- Taking a jumpseat.
- Accepting certain assignments.
- Relocating for reserve or a new contract.

Dispatch can care about current location because your pilot has to be physically positioned for work.

## Why the difference matters

You can be based at one airport and located at another.

Example:

- Your base is KATL.
- You finish a trip in KJFK.
- Your current location is now KJFK.
- The next assignment may require you to continue from KJFK or jumpseat back to base.

This makes Career Mode behave like a real roster instead of a loose route picker.

## Moving base

Move Base changes your assigned company base.

Before moving, review:

- Available bases.
- Cost.
- Route network.
- Employer fleet.
- Your type ratings.
- How the move affects future dispatch.

Moving base can cost money. Do not move unless it helps your long-term plan.

## Jumpseat

Jumpseat moves your current location without logging a flown Career assignment.

Use Jumpseat when you need to:

- Return to base.
- Reach an assignment origin.
- Handle reserve relocation.
- Position for a new contract.

For the full guide, see [Career Jumpseat](/career-jumpseat).

## Commuting and away-from-base expenses

Being away from base can create expenses, but short same-day turns are protected.

How it works:

- Your base is the airport your airline assigns you to.
- Your current location is where your pilot is right now.
- If those are different, FlyHub may consider you away from base.
- The first 12 hours away from base are free.
- After 12 hours, a capped incidental expense can apply.
- The cap is the lower of $300 or 15% of your reference weekly income.
- If you have accepted work starting from your current away airport, the away surcharge can be company-covered.

Returning to base ends the away period. If payroll already charged part of that same away period, returning to base only settles the remaining unpaid amount.

Do not automatically jumpseat back to base after every trip. If your next assignment starts from your current airport, staying there may be the better Career move.

For finance details, see [Career Finance](/career-finance#away-from-base-expenses) and [Career Payroll and Ledger](/career-payroll-ledger#away-from-base-ledger-entries).

## Common mistakes

- Assuming base and current location are always the same.
- Moving base without checking cost.
- Forgetting to jumpseat before an assignment.
- Looking for routes from base when your current location is somewhere else.
- Ignoring relocation requirements on reserve or job offers.
