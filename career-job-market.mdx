Job Market is where you look for your next Career opportunity.

Use it to compare airlines, apply for jobs, manage offers, complete interviews, and move into new contracts.

For a deeper walkthrough of application limits, interview windows, offer timers, and transfers, see [Career Applications and Offers](/career-applications-offers).

- Reputation requirements.

Use filters to find airlines that fit the aircraft, region, or career path you want.

## Requirements

Airlines can require minimum Career hours and reputation.

General tier requirements are:

| Airline tier | Typical requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 1-star | No minimum. |
| 2-star | 50 hours and 3.5 reputation. |
| 3-star | 150 hours and 5.5 reputation. |
| 4-star | 250 hours and 7.2 reputation. |
| 5-star | 400 hours and 8.6 reputation. |

Meeting the minimum requirement does not guarantee acceptance. Higher-tier airlines are more selective.

For a broader explanation of airline tiers, the pay formula, tier costs, pay markets and tax-free bases, see [Career Economy and Airline Tiers](/career-economy-tiers).

For how reputation is earned, lost, and used by airlines, see [Career Reputation](/career-reputation).

## Applying

### Returning after unemployment

After onboarding, an unemployed pilot receives one **Recovery Offer** when opening the Job Market. It does not use the monthly application allowance or require a reputation minimum. If you need training, the airline pays the full type-rating contribution and you complete the normal training program.

FlyHub picks the airline like this:

- **Close to you first.** Airlines in your country come first, then your region, then neighboring regions. Your country means where you are or are based, or your nationality (or chosen starter region). Whichever gives a closer match counts.
- **A size you've earned.** The offer can come from a 1★ airline up to the highest star tier whose hour and reputation requirements you already meet. Everyone can get up to 2★, and the limit is 3★. Within that range the size varies, so it isn't always the smallest airline. 4★ and 5★ airlines are reached through normal applications.
- **No repeats.** If you decline an offer, that airline moves to the back of the line. The next Recovery Offer appears 6 hours after a decline and comes from a different airline.
- **A usable job.** The airline must have a usable base and route network. An aircraft family you're already rated on is preferred.

If signing costs would leave you short of funds, choose **Sign with employer assistance** at signature. Your onboarding costs become an interest-free payroll advance and positioning to the selected base is included. Existing assistance debt carries forward. Existing employer rehire cooldowns still apply.

1. Open Job Market.
2. Filter airlines if needed.
3. Compare hour and reputation requirements.
4. Open airline details if you want more context.
5. Click Apply on an eligible airline.
6. Watch the offers panel for results.

## Application limits

Career applications have limits.

- You cannot apply to your current employer.
- If you are already employed, you usually need 30 days of continuous employment before applying elsewhere.
- You can submit up to 3 applications per month.
- Application reviews happen Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at 11:00 UTC.
- Rehire cooldowns can block some airlines.

Apply carefully. Do not waste applications on airlines that do not fit your progress.

## Acceptance chances

Meeting the listed minimums means you are eligible to apply. It does not mean the airline must accept you.

FlyHub weighs reputation, airline tier, and required hours.

General behavior:

- 9.0+ reputation gives very strong chances across all tiers, including 4-star and 5-star airlines.
- 8.0-8.9 reputation is strong, but 5-star airlines are still selective.
- 7.0-7.9 reputation works well for lower and mid-tier airlines, gives a moderate chance at 4-star airlines, and does not normally unlock 5-star acceptance.
- Below 6.9 reputation, 1-star to 3-star airlines remain possible, but 4-star and 5-star airlines are usually out of reach.
- Extra hours above the minimum can slightly improve the chance, especially for lower-tier airlines.

If you do not meet the required hours, the acceptance chance is 0%.

## Offers

Offers can come from:

- Your applications.
- Airlines reaching out passively.
- Promotions.
- Approved internal type-rating training offers.
- Recovery or fallback situations.

An offer can require:

- Interview.
- Contract review.
- Type-rating training.
- A type-rating price and training bond.
- Base selection.
- Relocation, medical, psychological, or transfer costs, minus the share the airline pays.
- A contract term choice.

Airline-initiated offers use a rolling market. Eligible pilots can receive up to 3 passive offers during any rolling 30-day period, with at least 7 days between offers and no more than 2 active external offers at once. Unopened external offers expire after 7 days so older cards do not block fresh opportunities.

## Interviews

Some offers require an interview.

Interviews have a limited time window once opened. The current interview and response window is 48 hours.

The interview has 10 questions and requires at least 5 correct answers to pass.

Your score can affect the salary multiplier on the offer. A weak but passing interview can reduce the salary, while a strong interview can improve it. Failing the interview can block or end the offer.

Question categories can include technical knowledge, CRM, regulations, operations, and weather.

## Type-rating costs

Type ratings are priced in hours of your own pay: 30 hours for a narrowbody, turboprop or regional type, 45 hours for a widebody, and 25% of that when you already hold a family that shares training. The offer shows the exact price in dollars and hours.

When an offer needs a new type, the airline pays the whole rating and you sign a 26-week training bond. If you leave early by resigning, moving to another airline or being dismissed, you repay the bond for the weeks left. Your first job carries no bond. Extra types you ask for yourself through an internal request in My Company are also paid by the airline, with no bond.

If the offer requires training, the contract shows the aircraft family, the rating price, the training bond, required trial flights, minimum score, pay, and base.

See [Type-rating price](/career-type-ratings-training#type-rating-price) and [Who pays for a rating](/career-type-ratings-training#who-pays-for-a-rating).

## Switching costs

Changing employers can include costs beyond training.

Depending on the offer, FlyHub can show:

- Relocation cost.
- Medical examination cost.
- Psychological examination cost.
- Transfer processing cost.

These costs scale with the airline tier and the airline's pay market. The offer's hiring package shows how much of each cost the airline pays and how much you pay. Airline-initiated offers always pay at least half of every cost.

Review the full offer before signing. You may need enough available balance to cover your share.

## Hiring packages, contract terms and counter-offers

Job Market offers can also include:

- A signing bonus, paid half at signature and half after 30 days on the line.
- A choice of Flexible (12 weeks), Standard (26 weeks) or Long-term (52 weeks) contract terms. Longer terms pay a bigger bonus and slightly higher pay but cost more to leave early.
- A counter-offer from your current airline when you open an outside offer. If the outside airline is a higher tier, your airline makes a [retention counter](/career-applications-offers#retention-counter) instead.

Some airlines are on a hiring drive each month and offer better packages. Your references from past airlines also affect your applications and packages.

See [Hiring packages](/career-applications-offers#hiring-packages), [Contract terms](/career-applications-offers#contract-terms), [Counter-offers](/career-applications-offers#counter-offers) and [References](/career-applications-offers#references) for the full rules.

## Contracts

When an offer is accepted, you may need to sign a contract.

Do not ignore pending contracts. A pending contract can lock dispatch until it is handled.

Review:

- Airline.
- Role.
- Pay.
- Base.
- Training obligations.
- Type-rating requirements.
- Penalties or terms.

Promotion offers also require a new contract package. A promotion contract keeps you with the same airline, updates your role, and activates the new pay terms after signature.

See [Career Contracts](/career-contracts) before signing any training, employment, promotion, or transfer package.

## Common mistakes

- Applying to airlines before meeting requirements.
- Applying to your current employer.
- Opening an offer and letting it expire.
- Ignoring an interview requirement.
- Forgetting that stronger reputation helps but does not guarantee acceptance.
- Signing a contract without checking base, aircraft, pay, and training obligations.
