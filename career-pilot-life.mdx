# Pilot Life and Personal Operations

**Pilot Life** is the practical part of Career Finance. It lets you spend money on the conditions around your flying: where you live, how you recover, how you handle illness, what the airline pays for, and which benefits you receive.

Money does not make a pilot invincible. It buys better odds, faster access to help, lower treatment costs, and more flexibility when plans go wrong.

Open it from **Career → Finance → Pilot life**.

## The simple loop

1. Check the duty light and the **Ready to fly?** row.
2. Look for the weakest condition: fatigue, health, sleep, stress, meals, or cash runway.
3. Open that system and compare the available choices.
4. Check **Price now**, **Your monthly**, **Balance after**, and **Runway after** before buying.
5. Equip the choice, or select **Save for this** to track one personal goal.
6. Fly, rest, treat an illness when needed, and check the ledger to see the result.

The **Best next dollar** recommendation points to the upgrade that helps the current bottleneck. It is a recommendation, not an automatic purchase. **If you do nothing** shows the hours and estimated pay that may be at risk.

## What the Finance page shows

| Area | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Duty light | Can I fly normally, should I be careful, or is a required issue blocking duty? |
| Cash cards | How much do I earn at the current hourly rate, spend each month, keep free, and have in runway? |
| Who covers this month | How much is paid by you, the airline, and union value. |
| Readiness board | Which condition is healthy, strained, or creating risk. |
| Pilot life board | What is equipped and what can be upgraded. |
| Employer package | What your current airline supplies on top of hourly pay. |
| Ledger | What actually posted to the account, including grouped weekly living costs. |

Projected pay is based on completed flight hours and is not guaranteed. There is no automatic sick pay: missing a flight means missing those hours.

## The seven systems

### Home

Your home affects recovery, sleep quality, stress, illness exposure, rent, and commute time.

| Choice | Trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| Crew lodging | Cheap and close, but noisier and tied to the employer and base. It can disappear when you leave the airline. |
| Standard rental | Flexible baseline housing at market rent. |
| Premium rental | Higher monthly cost for better sleep, lower stress, and better recovery. |
| Owned home | Down payment plus mortgage and maintenance. Strong recovery and equity, but less flexible when you move base. |

An owned home is a recovery asset, not a rental property. A rental property is an investment that produces income; it does not become your pilot's home or create a second recovery meter.

When you move base, review the preview before confirming. It shows the new rent, commute, home location, hotel package, union result, and runway. An owned home at another base can remain an asset while you pay for housing near the new base.

### Medical

Medical plans change the odds and cost of illness. They do not create immunity.

| Plan | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| No personal plan | Lowest fixed cost, but full treatment prices and normal access. |
| Basic cover | A small premium for lower treatment prices and slightly better illness odds. |
| Standard cover | A stronger balance of premium, treatment savings, and clinic access. |
| Premium aviation cover | Highest recurring cost, lowest treatment prices and illness exposure, with the fastest access. |

If you become sick:

1. The health status becomes **Sick** and the duty light can place you on a short health hold.
2. **Rest naturally** costs nothing, but duties inside the hold are missed and do not pay.
3. **Medicine** or **clinic treatment** can clear the hold immediately when appropriate. Severe illness may require a clinic rather than medicine.
4. If the clinic is more than your current balance, a **Medical advance** can clear the hold now and be repaid from future earnings with a surcharge.
5. After the hold, **Recovering** can temporarily reduce recovery speed.

The hold is intentionally short for a game. Money buys speed and access, not immunity. Keep a cash buffer because a severe illness costs more than a minor one.

Quarterly medical clearance is a separate requirement. A personal plan helps with illness treatment; it does not replace a required clearance payment.

### Hotels

When you are away from base for company flying, the airline provides a hotel standard based on its tier and region. A company layover is not the same as a personal away-from-base expense.

You can usually choose the company option or pay for a better rest stay when an upgrade is available. A better hotel improves rest and recovery for that stay; it does not erase fatigue or create a second fatigue meter.

The actual package is shown in **Employer package**. Higher airline tiers and some regions tend to provide better hotels, while union membership can raise the minimum hotel standard to a standard hotel.

### Meals

Meals influence recovery, stress, and illness exposure.

| Choice | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| No plan | Cheapest, but meals are inconsistent and can hurt recovery. |
| Basic meals | Covers a simple home routine; away-duty support may come from the airline. |
| Reliable meal plan | Prepared or dependable meals with steadier energy and recovery. |
| Premium meal access | Best nutrition support for a larger monthly commitment. |

FlyHub uses **daily meal allowance** for the money an airline provides for meals while you are away on duty. It is a work benefit, not salary, not a cash bonus for every flight, and not sick pay. The Employer package tells you whether it is supplied by the airline or added by the union.

### Transport

Transport changes commute time, lateness risk, stress, and fatigue gain.

| Choice | Trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| Public transit | No purchase cost, but the longest and least predictable commute. |
| Reliable personal transport | Upfront purchase and monthly running cost for a shorter, safer commute. |
| Premium low-friction transport | Highest cost and the smallest commute drag. |

Some airlines add a crew shuttle or reimburse commuting. That support appears in the Employer package and can reduce what you pay.

### Union

Union membership is a paid protection package, not a score multiplier or a replacement for personal planning.

It can provide:

- 15% off your remaining share of personal medical-plan premiums.
- A standard-hotel floor when the airline package is below standard.
- A daily meal allowance when the airline provides no meal support.
- One strike appeal per quarter.
- First-firing protection during the current employment, subject to the published rules.
- A small promotion adjustment where the promotion system allows it.

If the airline already provides a standard hotel or meal support, the union benefit may add little to that specific item. Finance shows **monthly dues**, **estimated monthly value**, and the number of active benefits so you can compare the package instead of guessing.

Membership requires an eligible active employment contract and opens after the required time with that employer. Dues scale with airline tier. Membership ends when you change employers, so review the new company's package before joining again.

Manage membership from **My Company**. See [My Company](/career-company#union) for eligibility and dues.

### Investments

Rental properties live on the **Investments** side of Finance. They are long-term income assets with a down payment, ongoing costs, and debt risk.

They do not improve sleep, remove illness, or replace a residence. Buy one only when the down payment still leaves room for treatment, loans, relocation, and normal living costs.

## Airline tier, region, and base

Your current airline package is calculated from more than the hourly rate:

| Factor | Changes |
| --- | --- |
| Airline star tier | Pay band, hotel standard, medical contribution, meal support, transport support, and union dues. |
| Airline region | Pay and local cost levels, plus regional hotel, meal, transport, or medical norms. |
| Base | Local rent, commute, taxes, and the practical value of your home and transport. |
| Union status | Medical discount, hotel and meal floors, dues, appeals, and employment protection. |

After an employer change, the new airline package is recalculated. Employer hotel, medical contribution, meal support, transport support, leave policy, and union membership belong to the new job. Personal choices and owned assets remain part of the save, but their price and usefulness can change with the new base and market.

This is why a higher-paying job is not automatically the better offer. Compare the complete package and the runway after the move in the Employer and Finance previews.

For the full economy rules, see [Career Economy and Airline Tiers](/career-economy-tiers). For moving, see [Career Base and Current Location](/career-base-location).

## How status works

The readiness board is a quick operating view:

- **GO**: no current issue is blocking normal duty.
- **ADVISORY**: you can usually fly, but a condition is increasing fatigue, stress, cost, or illness exposure.
- **HOLD**: a required issue must be fixed before duty can continue.

Fatigue, sleep, stress, meals, and cash runway create soft consequences. They do not independently lock you out for days. Active illness, overdue medical clearance, or a failed or missed line check can create a true hold.

## A good spending order

There is no single perfect build, but this order is usually easy to understand:

1. Keep enough cash for a medical event and the next recurring payments.
2. Fix a true hold before buying comfort.
3. Improve the weakest readiness row, not the most expensive row.
4. Buy transport when commute time or lateness risk is the problem.
5. Upgrade home, meals, or hotels when recovery and stress are costing more than the monthly price.
6. Join the union when its active benefits are worth more to your current airline than the dues.
7. Buy rental property only after the career account can absorb the down payment and debt.

## How the ledger presents it

The Account ledger can group recurring weekly living costs into one row, such as **Weekly living costs · 7 items**. Select the group to see the receipt-style breakdown and each original balance.

Grouping changes only the presentation. It does not change transactions, balances, payroll, or calculations. Salary, taxes, loan payments, medical charges, union dues, property purchases, and other non-living entries remain visible as individual activity.
