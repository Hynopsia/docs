# Career Applications and Offers

Applications and offers are how you move between airlines in Career Mode.

Use Job Market when you want a new employer, better pay, a different fleet, or a different region.

## Application basics

Airlines can require:

- Minimum Career hours.
- Minimum reputation.
- Suitable type rating.
- Training availability.
- Open application eligibility.

Meeting the minimums makes you eligible to apply. It does not guarantee acceptance.

## Application limits

Current application rules include:

- You can apply to up to 3 airlines per month.
- You cannot apply to your current employer.
- If employed, you normally need at least 30 days with the current airline before applying elsewhere.
- Application reviews start Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at 11:00 UTC for your active save. This schedule follows real UTC time, even if your Career calendar is behind. Paused saves are skipped; pending applications can be reviewed after you reactivate the save.
- Some airlines can be blocked by rehire cooldown after a firing or failed employment outcome.

Apply carefully. Wasting an application can slow down your month.

## Acceptance chance

FlyHub weighs hours, reputation, airline tier, and fit.

General behavior:

- High reputation improves your chance.
- Extra hours above the minimum can help.
- Higher-tier airlines are more selective.
- If you do not meet the required hours, your acceptance chance is 0%.
- Poor reputation makes 4-star and 5-star airlines very unlikely.
- References from your previous airlines can move your chance by up to 6% either way. See [References](#references).

For tier requirements, see [Career Economy and Airline Tiers](/career-economy-tiers).

## Interviews

Some offers require an interview.

The interview has:

- 10 questions.
- A 48-hour window after it is opened.
- A minimum passing score of 5 correct answers.

Interview topics can include:

- Technical knowledge.
- CRM.
- Regulations.
- Operations.
- Weather.

Your interview result can affect the salary multiplier. A strong interview can improve the offer. A weak but passing interview can reduce the offer. A failed interview can end the opportunity.

## Offer timers

Some offers expire.

External market offers remain available unopened for 7 days. Opening one starts a 48-hour response and interview window, so do not open it unless you are ready to review it.

Before the timer expires, decide whether to:

- Accept the offer.
- Complete the interview.
- Arrange money for training costs.
- Take a loan if available.
- Decline the offer.

## What an offer can include

An offer can include:

- Airline.
- Rank.
- Base.
- Aircraft family.
- Hourly pay.
- Training requirement.
- Type-rating cost share.
- Signing bonus.
- Relocation cost.
- Medical examination cost.
- Psychological examination cost.
- Processing cost.
- How much of each cost the airline pays.
- Contract term.
- Contract signature.

Read every line before accepting.

## Hiring packages

Offers from the Job Market come with a hiring package. It shows what the airline is willing to do to bring you on board.

Offer rows in Job Market show short tags such as **$10k bonus**, **Moving paid**, **All costs paid**, **Hiring drive**, or **Counter-offer**. Open the offer to see the full package.

### Signing bonus

Some offers include a signing bonus. The better your profile, the more likely you are to get one and the bigger it is.

FlyHub looks at:

- Your reputation above the airline's minimum.
- Your hours above the airline's minimum.
- Whether you already hold a type rating on their fleet.
- Your recent flight scores.
- Your references.

The airline tier sets the top bonus:

| Airline tier | Top First Officer bonus | Top Captain bonus |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1-star | $5,000 | $7,500 |
| 2-star | $10,000 | $15,000 |
| 3-star | $15,000 | $22,500 |
| 4-star | $20,000 | $30,000 |
| 5-star | $25,000 | $30,000 |

Regional pay levels adjust the amount, and bonuses come in $2,500 steps. No signing bonus is ever above $30,000, even with a longer contract term.

How the bonus is paid:

- Half is paid when you sign the contract.
- The other half is paid after 30 days on the line, once any training is finished and your employment contract is active.
- Each airline pays you a signing bonus only once per Career save. Returning to the same airline later does not pay another one.

Airlines that sent you the offer themselves are more likely to include a bonus than offers you got by applying.

### Who pays the switching costs

Every move has four costs: relocation, medical exam, psychological exam, and processing. The package shows each cost, the share the airline pays, and the share you pay.

- Airline-initiated offers always pay at least half of every cost, and often all of it.
- Offers you got by applying usually leave more of the cost to you. A strong profile still gets better coverage.

Switching costs scale with the airline tier and the region's pay level, so a move costs a similar share of salary at every tier. A 1-star move in a low-pay region is much cheaper than a 5-star move in North America.

### Hiring drives

Each month, some airlines are on a hiring drive. This is the same for every pilot. During a drive, the airline is more likely to offer a bonus and pays more of your moving costs. Job Market marks these airlines with **Hiring drive this month**.

### Former employers

If you flew for the airline before, your reference from them matters:

- A good or excellent reference improves the package.
- A poor or negative reference means no signing bonus from that airline.

## Contract terms

When you sign a Job Market offer, you choose how long you commit to the airline.

| Term | Length | Bonus | Pay | Leaving early |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flexible | 12 weeks | Base bonus | Base pay | Free after 30 days, same as before |
| Standard | 26 weeks | Bonus ×1.25 plus 1 week of pay | +2% | Up to 2 weeks of pay, capped at $10,000 |
| Long-term | 52 weeks | Bonus ×1.5 plus 2 weeks of pay | +4% | Up to 4 weeks of pay, capped at $20,000 |

Standard and Long-term terms add the extra weeks of pay to your bonus even when the offer has no signing bonus.

The early-exit penalty shrinks as you get closer to the end of the term, and leaving early also costs some reputation. It applies when you resign, and also when you leave for another airline's offer before the term ends.

### Repaying a bonus

If you leave before your term ends, you repay the part of your bonus you have not earned yet. The share shrinks over the term: leave halfway through and you repay about half.

You also repay unearned bonuses if you are dismissed for strikes or inactivity. You do not repay anything if your airline shuts down.

When you review an outside offer, the contract step shows what leaving your current airline would cost right now. The resignation dialog in My Company shows the same numbers.

## Counter-offers

When you open an outside offer, your current airline may try to keep you.

A counter-offer can include:

- A raise of 3% to 12%.
- Sometimes a retention bonus of up to $15,000.

Your airline only counters when:

- You have been with them for at least 60 days.
- The outside offer pays more than your current rate.
- You are not in training.
- You have not accepted a counter-offer in the last 180 days.

The higher your reputation, recent scores and tenure, the more likely your airline is to counter, and the more it offers.

The counter-offer appears next to the outside offer. Choose **Stay with** your airline to accept it. Your new pay starts right away, you keep your seniority and base, and the outside offer is declined. A retention bonus comes with a 26-week commitment, and leaving sooner means repaying part of it.

If you accept the outside offer instead, the counter-offer is declined. It also ends when the outside offer expires.

## References

Every time you leave an airline, it gives you a reference.

| Grade | Usually earned by |
| --- | --- |
| Excellent | Staying a year or more and finishing your contract term. |
| Good | Staying 120 days or more and finishing your term, or losing your job because the airline shut down. |
| Neutral | Leaving after a short stay, or leaving in the second half of your term. |
| Poor | Leaving within 30 days, leaving in the first half of your term, or not passing training. |
| Negative | Being dismissed for strikes or inactivity. |

Your overall grade weighs your most recent airlines the most, so a bad exit fades as you build a better record. Your references:

- Change your application chance by up to 6% either way.
- Shape the hiring packages airlines offer you.
- Matter most when you go back to an airline you have flown for before.

Job Market shows your overall reference grade next to your monthly applications. Promotions and training completion at the same airline do not count as leaving.

## Same type or new type

If the airline operates a type you already hold, you may be able to transfer into line work faster.

If the offer requires a new type rating, you may need to:

- Pay the displayed family price for external training.
- Sign a training contract.
- Complete training flights.
- Meet the required average score.
- Sign the employment contract after training.

For training rules, see [Career Type Ratings and Training](/career-type-ratings-training).

Internal type-rating requests are separate from Job Market applications. Once you reach the hours target, you start the request from My Company; if the airline approves it, the training offer appears for you to open. Internal training is company-paid, while external market training normally remains your responsibility.

## Passive job offers

Airlines can also reach out with job offers when your Career profile fits them.

These offers are meant to feel like recruiters noticing your hours, reputation, type ratings, and current pay. They can still include timers, costs, interviews, or training requirements. Because the airline came to you, it always pays at least half of every switching cost and is more likely to include a signing bonus. See [Hiring packages](#hiring-packages).

The passive market has the same opportunity ceiling for every eligible pilot: up to 3 generated offers in a rolling 30-day period, at least 7 days between generated offers, and no more than 2 active external offers at once. Most offers match a type rating you already hold. Some are training-track offers that clearly show the additional qualification path.

## Transfer outcome

If you accept a transfer:

- Your new employer becomes active after the contract process.
- Your seniority and tenure start again at the new airline.
- Your rank depends on the new airline's tier. See [Rank when you change airline](/career-ranks-promotions#rank-when-you-change-airline).
- Your base can change.
- Pending flights from the previous employer can be cleared.
- Costs can be deducted from your Career balance.

## Common mistakes

- Applying before meeting requirements.
- Opening offers before you are ready.
- Ignoring the interview timer.
- Accepting a transfer without checking what you pay after the airline's share of relocation and medical costs.
- Choosing a Long-term contract and then leaving early, which costs a penalty and part of your bonus.
- Accepting an outside offer without checking whether your current airline made a counter-offer.
- Assuming higher-tier means automatically better for your current type ratings.
- Forgetting that new type training can lock Dispatch to that training family.
