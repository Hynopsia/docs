Career Mode economy is built around airline tier, region, rank, contract status, and your pilot history.

Airline tier is important, but it is not the only factor. A 5-star airline usually pays more than a 1-star airline, but it also has higher entry requirements and higher union dues.

## Airline tiers

FlyHub uses 1-star to 5-star airline tiers.

In general:

| Tier | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| 1-star | Entry-level airlines. Easier to access, lower pay, and good for starting a career. |
| 2-star | Early progression airlines. Some requirements, better pay, and still forgiving for junior pilots. |
| 3-star | Mid-career airlines. Higher requirements and stronger pay. |
| 4-star | Advanced airlines. Strong reputation and hours are expected. |
| 5-star | Top-tier airlines. Highest pay potential and strictest requirements. |

Not every airline in the same tier feels identical. Fleet, route network, region, base choices, and available aircraft families still matter.

## Job tier requirements

These are the baseline Career requirements for applying to airline tiers.

| Airline tier | Career hours | Reputation |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1-star | 0 | 0.0 |
| 2-star | 50 | 3.5 |
| 3-star | 150 | 5.5 |
| 4-star | 250 | 7.2 |
| 5-star | 400 | 8.6 |

Some airlines can have explicit requirements above the baseline. FlyHub uses the higher value.

Meeting the requirement only means you can apply. Acceptance still depends on reputation, hours, airline tier, and application outcome.

See [Career Job Market](/career-job-market#acceptance-chances) for the application chance guide.

## How your pay is calculated

Career pay is one formula. There are no random pay ranges: two pilots in the same seat at the same airline earn the same contract rate, and everything above that is earned.

```text
contract rate = tier base × rank × region
you earn      = contract rate × (1 + seniority + union) × performance
```

Your [pay slip](/career-payroll-ledger#how-your-rate-is-built) shows every part of this formula for your own contract, plus the next ways your rate goes up.

### Tier base and rank

Hourly contract rates before the pay market factor:

| Tier | First Officer | Senior First Officer | Captain | Senior Captain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1-star | $57.80 | $66.30 | $85.00 | $95.20 |
| 2-star | $79.56 | $91.26 | $117.00 | $131.04 |
| 3-star | $102.00 | $117.00 | $150.00 | $168.00 |
| 4-star | $136.00 | $156.00 | $200.00 | $224.00 |
| 5-star | $180.20 | $206.70 | $265.00 | $296.80 |

Each rank earns a share of the Captain rate: First Officer 68%, Senior First Officer 78%, Captain 100%, Senior Captain 112%. A promotion is always a real raise, and promotion offers are at least 10% above your current rate.

Each tier pays about 30% more than the one below it. A 5-star Captain earns about 1.8 times a 3-star Captain, so a move to a major is a big step up without being a cliff.

FlyHub shows yearly figures using 900 paid hours a year. A 5-star Captain contract is about $238,500 a year before region, seniority and performance.

The job board shows each offer's pay market, its tax, and about how much you keep each week after tax, so you can compare a tax-free Gulf offer with a higher gross offer elsewhere.

### What changes your contract rate

- **Interview.** Your interview score sets the offer between 95% and 105% of the formula. One quiz never defines a career.
- **Contract Negotiation and related Pilot Skills.** They raise the pay on new offers.
- **Training contracts.** Market training pays 50% of the rate while training is active.

Stored airline pay profiles no longer change pay: every airline of the same tier uses the same formula.

## What progression costs

Skills, type ratings, line-check rechecks and base moves are priced in hours of your own pay, so a goal costs the same flying at every tier. Housing, property and airline stakes stay in dollars. See [What progression costs](/career-finance#what-progression-costs).

## Pilot Pension

Every payday, 5% of your weekly base pay goes into your pension. Higher-tier airlines add a bigger match: 0% at 1-star, 1% at 2-star, 2% at 3-star, 4% at 4-star and 5% at 5-star. The match vests after 8 weeks at the airline. See [Career Pilot Pension](/career-pension).

## Living costs and taxes

Living costs and taxes follow the country of your base:

- **Living costs** use your base's pay market (see the table above). Big hubs such as New York, London, Paris, Zurich, Dubai, Tokyo, Hong Kong, Singapore and Sydney cost more again.
- **Tax and social contributions** start from the market's typical rate, or the country's own rate where FlyHub has one (for example the US, UK, Germany, France and Australia). Higher incomes then add up to a few percentage points. Tax never goes above 28%.

### Tax-free bases

Some countries have no personal income tax. When your base is in one of them, your weekly payroll has **no income tax at all**, and your pay slip says so:

- The Gulf: United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait.
- Brunei and Monaco.
- The Bahamas, Bermuda, the Cayman Islands, the British Virgin Islands, Turks and Caicos, Anguilla, and Vanuatu.

Tax-free does not mean cheap: Gulf hubs such as Dubai and Doha are high-cost bases, so rent and daily costs are higher. The gross pay factor is a little lower than the United States, but take-home is usually higher.

## Protected weekly take-home

Normal mandatory deductions are capped at 25% of gross weekly payroll.

The cap combines:

- Tax or social contributions.
- Rent or housing.
- Normal living costs.
- Normal commuting.

This protects at least 75% of gross payroll before separate obligations. For example, $5,390 gross pay allows no more than $1,347.50 in these combined deductions, leaving at least $4,042.50 before other costs.

Loans, voluntary union dues, penalties, property purchases, medical charges, and personal events are outside this cap. They can still reduce your final balance because they are separate Career obligations or decisions.

## Starter economy relief

FlyHub gives extra economy relief to junior pilots in entry-level employment.

Starter economy relief applies when:

- You are not in training.
- You work for a 1-star airline.
- Your role is First Officer or Senior First Officer.

When eligible:

- Housing cost is reduced by 40%.
- Discretionary living costs are reduced to 65% of normal.

This helps new Career pilots survive the early grind without removing the need to manage money.

## Training pay

Training contracts from normal market offers can pay 50% of the full employment rate while training is active.

Internal type-rating requests are different. If your current employer approves the request, you keep your current salary during training, but you pay for that extra rating yourself. Ratings the airline needs you on are paid by the airline under a 26-week training bond. See [Who pays for a rating](/career-type-ratings-training#who-pays-for-a-rating).

### Pay protection after the 2026 rebalance

When Career pay was rebalanced in November 2026, nobody lost more than 15% on the day. If your new contract rate was lower than that, you keep a **protected rate** that steps down by up to 10% each payday until it reaches your new contract rate. Only weeks you fly count, so time away never uses up your protection. Your pay slip shows the protected rate and how many paydays are left. A promotion never lowers your pay: protection carries into the new rank and keeps stepping down toward the higher rank's rate. Moving to another airline ends protection and pays the new formula right away.

## Seniority pay

Every 8 weeks of continuous employment at your airline adds 1% to your pay, up to 10%. The **Seniority** Pilot Skill makes a step every 7 weeks, and at rank 2 it lifts the cap to 13%.

Seniority belongs to your airline. Changing employers resets it, and moving up more than one tier can cost you rank, so a higher hourly rate is not the only thing to consider. That is the real choice in Career: stay and build seniority, or jump to a better airline and start again. See [Rank when you change airline](/career-ranks-promotions#rank-when-you-change-airline).

## Performance pay

Performance pay rewards how you fly, and it **follows you to every airline**.

Every weekly performance review moves it:

| Week's average score | Change |
| --- | ---: |
| 9.0 or higher | +1% |
| 8.0 to 8.9 | +0.5% |
| 7.5 to 7.9 | No change |
| Below 7.5 | -1% |

It stays between 92% and 110% of your rate. A week without Career flights changes nothing. The weekly performance report tells you how it moved.

## Minimum credit and the weekly guarantee

- **Every leg pays at least 1 hour.** A 25-minute hop pays an hour, so short regional and Caravan legs are worth flying.
- **Fly at least one leg in a pay week and you are paid for at least 4 hours.** The guarantee tops up your weekly pay at your current rate. A week without flights pays nothing extra.

## Extra pay per flight

Stacking bonuses on a leg (the on-time bonus and Special Airports pay) are added on top of its base pay. Together they can add up to **60% of that leg's base pay**. If they stack above that, each is scaled down so the total is exactly 60%. Reserve calls keep their full 2.0x duty rate, and flat payments such as adverse-weather hazard pay and weekly stipends are not part of the cap.

## Union dues

Union dues are charged monthly at 1.8% of your contract base pay, with a minimum of $40 and a maximum of $600 a month. Higher-tier airlines pay more, so their dues are higher too, but dues stay a small share of your salary at every tier.

Members also earn a union pay step: +1% hourly pay for every 2 months of continuous membership with the same airline, up to 5%. It adds to your seniority pay. Membership also helps with strikes, firing protection, promotion requirements and recall after an inactivity dismissal.


See [My Company](/career-company#union) for the full union guide.

## Early resignation penalties

Your contract term sets how long the early resignation penalty applies.

On a **Flexible** contract, and on contracts signed before contract terms existed, FlyHub applies an early resignation penalty if you resign before 30 days of continuous employment.

The maximum is the lower of $5,000 or one week of expected gross pay. FlyHub then multiplies that amount by the days remaining in the 30-day commitment.

| When you resign | Remaining share of maximum penalty | Maximum reputation loss |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Immediately | 100% | -0.50 |
| After 10 days | 66.67% | -0.33 |
| After 20 days | 33.33% | -0.17 |
| After 30 days | 0% | 0 |

After 30 days, the early resignation money and reputation penalties no longer apply on a Flexible contract.

Longer terms work the same way over a longer window, with a higher maximum:

| Term | Penalty window | Maximum penalty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flexible | 30 days | Lower of $5,000 or 1 week of pay |
| Standard | 26 weeks | Lower of $10,000 or 2 weeks of pay |
| Long-term | 52 weeks | Lower of $20,000 or 4 weeks of pay |

On Standard and Long-term contracts, the penalty also applies when you leave early for another airline's offer.

Leaving before your term ends also means repaying the unearned part of any signing or retention bonus. See [Contract terms](/career-applications-offers#contract-terms).

## Switching costs by tier

Relocation, medical, psychological and processing costs scale with the airline tier and the airline's pay market. Moving to a 1-star airline in a low-pay market costs a few thousand dollars; moving to a 5-star airline in North America can cost well over $10,000.

The hiring package on each offer shows how much of that the airline pays. Airline-initiated offers always pay at least half of every cost. See [Hiring packages](/career-applications-offers#hiring-packages).

## Medical check costs

Medical checks are recurring Career costs.

Current ranges are:

| Situation | Medical check range |
| --- | ---: |
| Starter relief pilot | $175-$400 |
| 2-star junior FO or Senior FO | $225-$550 |
| Standard range | $250-$700 |

If a medical check becomes overdue and cannot be paid, Career duty can be grounded until the payment is handled.

## Property investments

Property investments are part of the Career economy and apply only to Career Mode.

| Property tier | Down payment | Monthly net income | Most you can own |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Starter Duplex | $10,000 | $600 | 2 |
| Small Apartment Block | $35,000 | $2,275 | 1 |
| Urban Mixed-Use Complex | $90,000 | $6,300 | 1 |

Each tier pays its down payment back in roughly 13 to 17 real weeks. The ownership limits keep rental income a bonus on top of flying pay.

Property can create passive income, but buying too early can leave you short on cash for training, medical checks, relocation, or loan payments.

See [Career Finance](/career-finance) for loans, credit, property, and ledger details.

## What tier does not control

Tier does not directly replace:

- Type ratings.
- Contract signature requirements.
- Dispatch locks.
- Training status.
- Current location.
- Medical clearance.
- Line checks.
- Tracking method.

For actual flight tracking, Career Mode uses the shared FlyHub tracking systems. See [Tracking Methods](/tracking-methods), [Online Tracking](/online-tracking), and [Manual and Offline Mode](/manual-mode).
