---

Every Career pilot has a **Pilot Pension**. Each payday, part of your pay goes into it, your airline can add a match, and the money you own grows a little every week. You can borrow from it, use it for your first home, lean on it when you cannot work, or keep it for the day your pilot retires.

Open it from **Finance → Investments → Pension**.

## How money goes in

| Part | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Your savings | 5% of your weekly base pay goes into the pension every payday. Your pay slip shows it as its own line. |
| Airline match | Your airline adds a share of the same pay, by its star tier (see below). |
| Growth | 0.08% a week on the money that is already yours, about 4% a year. |

### Airline match by tier

| Airline tier | Match |
| --- | ---: |
| 1-star | 0% |
| 2-star | 1% |
| 3-star | 2% |
| 4-star | 4% |
| 5-star | 5% |

A 5-star airline doubles what you put in. A 1-star airline adds nothing, so your pension grows only from your own savings early in your career.

### When the match becomes yours

The match **vests after 8 weeks** at that airline. Until then it sits under **Waiting to vest**. Once you pass 8 weeks, everything waiting moves into your balance and every later match is yours straight away.

If you leave the airline before 8 weeks, the match waiting there is lost. Your own savings, their growth and any match that already vested always stay with you.

The panel shows how many weeks you have left before the match vests.

## What you can do with it

Only the money that is yours today counts: your savings, their growth and the vested match. Withdrawals take your own savings first. While you owe money on a pension loan, the same amount stays locked in your pension.

| Option | What it does | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First home deposit** | Puts pension money toward the down payment when you buy an owned home in Pilot Life. Once per save, up to the full down payment. If you sell the home, that money goes back into your pension. | None |
| **Borrow from yourself** | A loan of up to 50% of your balance, paid back over 26 weekly paydays into your pension. | 0%, no fees, no credit check |
| **Hardship withdrawal** | Takes money out while you cannot work. | None |
| **Cash out early** | Takes money out at any time. | 30% penalty, then income tax on the rest |
| **Retire** | Ends this Career for good and turns part of your pension into a head start for your next pilot. | See [Retirement](#retirement) |

### Borrowing from yourself

- You can hold one pension loan at a time, from $100 up to half of your balance.
- It is repaid in 26 equal weekly payments, taken from your bank account on payday and put back into your pension.
- It does not touch your credit score and does not count as a bank loan.
- If your bank balance cannot cover a payment, that week is skipped. There is no fee and no credit hit, but the loan runs a week longer. You get a notice, and the panel shows how many weeks behind you are.
- What you still owe is locked in your pension. You cannot cash it out, take it as a hardship withdrawal or use it for a home deposit until it is repaid.
- If you retire with a loan open, what you still owe is taken off your pension before the legacy credit is worked out.

### First home deposit

Pension money you put into a home is tied to that home. When you sell it, that share of the sale goes back into your pension instead of your bank account, less the same 4% selling fee as the rest of the sale. The sale preview shows both amounts before you confirm.

### Hardship withdrawal

You can take a hardship withdrawal when you cannot work:

- You are grounded because your medical has lapsed.
- You are unemployed after being dismissed.
- You are unemployed after your airline shut down.

Each of these lets you take up to **4 weeks of your last weekly pay**, with no penalty and no tax. The same limit also covers any 13 weeks: all your hardship withdrawals in that time cannot add up to more than 4 weeks of pay, even across different groundings.

Sick leave you ask for and resigning on your own do not count.

### Cashing out early

You can take money out at any time, but it is expensive. 30% is lost as a penalty, and income tax is charged on what is left. The panel shows what you would take home before you confirm.

Example: cash out $10,000 at a 15% tax rate. The penalty is $3,000, tax is $1,050, and you take home $5,950.

## Retirement

You can retire your pilot once all three are true:

- You are a **Captain or Senior Captain** at an airline.
- Your Career has run for **26 active weeks** since induction. Time the save spends paused doesn't count.
- Your logbook has **500 hours**.

The **Retire** row in the Pension panel shows your progress on each.

1. Open **Finance → Investments → Pension** and choose **Retire**.
2. Review your title and your pension at retirement.
3. Type `RETIRE` to confirm.

When you retire:

- Your profile shows a permanent title with your rank, your airline and the hours you flew, for example **Retired Senior Captain, British Airways · 2,140 h**. Each pilot you retire adds their own title.
- The save is closed and can never be loaded again. It moves to **Retired pilots** in the career chooser, outside your three slots, so its slot is free for a new pilot. Its logbook is kept for good and can't be deleted.
- Any open pension loan closes with the pension. What you still owe is taken off first.
- You earn a one-time **legacy credit**: 25% of your pension at retirement (after any open loan), up to $25,000.

The next new Career save you create starts with the legacy credit if it is more than the standard opening balance for your difficulty. If the standard balance is larger, you start with that and your credit is kept for a later Career.

You cannot retire while a flight is open or while your Career is still saving something. Wait a moment and try again.

Retiring cannot be undone. Think of it as finishing a career you are proud of, not as a way out of a bad week.

## On your pay slip and ledger

| Ledger entry | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Pension: 5% of this week's pay | Your weekly savings, taken from payroll. |
| Pension loan | Money borrowed from your pension, paid into your bank. |
| Pension loan repayment | This week's repayment, back into your pension. |
| Pension hardship withdrawal | Money taken out while you cannot work, with no penalty. |
| Pension cash-out | An early cash-out, showing the penalty and tax. |

Your weekly **Payroll deposited** notice also has a pension line with what you saved, the airline match and the growth for that payday.

If you resign while a match is still waiting to vest, the resignation screen tells you how much you would lose and the date to stay until to keep it.

## Common mistakes

- Changing airline a week before the match vests. Check **Waiting to vest** before you accept an offer.
- Cashing out when a pension loan or hardship withdrawal would cost nothing.
- Forgetting the first-home deposit when you buy a home in [Pilot Life](/career-pilot-life).
- Retiring before your pension is big enough to give the legacy credit you want.

For the rest of your money, see [Career Finance](/career-finance) and [Career Payroll and Ledger](/career-payroll-ledger).
