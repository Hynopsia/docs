Career Finance is where you manage money in Career Mode.

Use it to understand what already happened, what costs are coming, and whether you can afford loans, relocation, property, or career moves.

## Finance tabs

Finance has three tabs:

| Tab | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Pilot life | Readiness, your monthly budget, home, medical cover, meals, transport, union, and what your airline covers. |
| Investments | Planning around balance, cash flow, debt, credit, loans, property, and your next paycheck. |
| Ledger | Reviewing past transactions and ledger history. |

## Ledger

The Ledger tab can show:

- Lifetime income.
- Lifetime expenses.
- Net career flow.
- Peak balance.
- Record count.
- First record date.
- Account ledger.

Use the Ledger when you want to audit what happened.

Examples:

- Salary payment.
- Loan payment.
- Medical check.
- Union dues.
- Property income.
- Penalty.
- Expense.

## Investments

The Investments tab is for planning and financial decisions. From top to bottom it shows:

| Section | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Balance | Your cash now and one column per day for the last 30 days. Hover a day to see its closing balance, net change, and biggest transaction. |
| Debt | What you owe, how much is paid off, your monthly payments, credit score, and the date your last loan ends. |
| Income | Your hourly flight pay and your recent paychecks. Hover a paycheck for its date and amount. |
| Monthly budget | A normal month laid out like a load sheet: pay at the top, each cost taking its share, and what is left over at the bottom. |
| Reserve | How many months of costs your cash covers if pay stopped, measured against the 1-month and 3-month marks. |
| Next paycheck | The current pay week with your flights on it, your take-home pay slip, and the flights that will be paid. Hover a flight on the week strip or in the list to match them up. |
| Credit and loans | Your credit score, which loan each score unlocks, the loan offers, and the loans you hold. |
| Property | Rental properties you can buy and what each one pays you. |

## Pilot life

The first part of Finance is **Pilot life**, also called Personal Operations. It turns your balance into practical career choices: home, medical cover, away-from-base rest, meals, transport, union membership, and investments.

Use it to answer:

- Am I ready to fly, or is something putting me on hold?
- What is my weakest readiness condition?
- What does the airline or union already cover?
- What would the next upgrade cost now and every month?
- How much runway would remain after buying it?

The page shows a duty light, a **Who covers this month** split, a **Best next dollar** recommendation, and the cost of doing nothing when hours may be at risk. It is a planning tool: it does not buy an upgrade automatically.

For the full system guide, see [Pilot Life and Personal Operations](/career-pilot-life).

## Payroll

Career payroll is due Monday at 13:00 on the selected save's Career clock. Pausing that save pauses the time left until payday, so payment may fall on a different real-world day. See [Payroll timing](/career-payroll-ledger#payroll-timing) for an example.

Your pay can depend on:

- Contract.
- Role.
- Career flight hours.
- Training status.
- Seniority.
- Bid-board or special bonuses.

Training pay can be lower than full employment pay.

Training contracts from normal market offers can pay 50% of the employment rate while you complete the training program. Approved internal type-rating requests are company-paid and keep your current salary during training.

Career pay scales are based on airline tier, region, and rank. Higher-tier airlines generally pay more, and region affects the final pay band. North America, Europe, the Middle East, and Oceania trend higher than South America and Africa. Rank also matters: Senior First Officer and Senior Captain use higher pay multipliers than the base seat level.

Every 8 weeks of continuous employment can add a seniority bonus to pay, up to the system cap.

See [Career Economy and Airline Tiers](/career-economy-tiers) for the full tier, region, pay band, and cost guide.

See [Career Payroll and Ledger](/career-payroll-ledger) for the full payroll, bonus, deduction, and ledger glossary.

Costs can include:

- Rent or housing.
- Food and groceries.
- Transport.
- Utilities.
- Subscriptions.
- Social spending.

Use Finance before making decisions that cost money.

## Protected weekly take-home

Tax, housing, normal living costs, and normal commuting cannot exceed 25% of gross weekly payroll when combined.

This protects at least 75% of gross payroll before separate obligations. The detailed expense categories remain visible in your ledger even when FlyHub reduces their applied total.

Loan payments, voluntary union dues, penalties, property purchases, medical charges, and personal events are outside this protection and can reduce your final take-home further.

## Away-from-base expenses

Career Mode tracks your base and your current location separately. Your base is your assigned company station. Your current location is where your pilot is right now.

The airline covers normal company layovers. If airline-assigned flying leaves you away from base overnight, FlyHub does not charge your Career balance just because you needed a hotel or rest at another airport.

A small personal away-from-base cost can apply only when all of these are true:

- You have been away from base for more than 48 hours.
- You do not have accepted company work starting from your current away airport.
- You do not have an active or upcoming Career booking from that airport.
- The time away is effectively personal positioning, not a company-required layover.

When it applies, the cost is intentionally limited. The first 48 hours are free, then FlyHub uses a small hourly personal-overstay rate and caps the charge at the lower of $150 or 10% of your reference weekly income.

Examples:

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| You fly from base to another airport and return a few minutes later. | No away-from-base expense. |
| You fly from base to another airport and return the same day. | No away-from-base expense. |
| You stay overnight at another airport because of company flying. | The airline covers the layover. |
| You are away from base because your next accepted assignment starts there. | The away time is company-covered. |
| You remain away from base for more than 48 hours with no company work from that airport. | A small capped personal overstay cost can apply. |
| You choose to jumpseat somewhere. | The jumpseat ticket can cost money unless it is airline-sponsored. |

Returning to base can still be useful because it changes your current location and may make future Dispatch options easier. But do not return only because you expect a normal company-layover hotel bill.

For base and current location details, see [Career Base and Current Location](/career-base-location). For repositioning, see [Career Jumpseat](/career-jumpseat).

## Medical checks

Medical clearance can affect whether you can fly.

If a medical check is due, pay it from Finance when the app allows it.

Ignoring medical clearance can block Career dispatch.

Medical checks run on a recurring cycle after training. FlyHub warns you before the due date. If the check becomes overdue and cannot be paid, Career flight duty can be grounded until the medical payment is handled.

Medical check costs vary by career stage and airline tier. Starter and junior pilots can see lower ranges, while normal checks can cost several hundred dollars.

## Employer assistance after unemployment

If you have completed Career onboarding and are unemployed, essential signing costs can be deferred when paying them would leave less than $500. Select **Sign with employer assistance** on an eligible contract. This does not require bank credit or a minimum reputation score.

The airline funds relocation, medical and psychological exams, and processing directly. Nothing is paid into your account, your existing cash stays available, and signing positions you at the new base. Assistance does not fund voluntary type-rating purchases; the Job Market provides a return-to-work offer with fully sponsored training if you need it.

The advance is interest-free and repaid through up to 10% of positive earned take-home pay after other payroll deductions. Savings and property income do not fund these deductions. No earned pay means no payment, interest, late fees, or reputation penalty from assistance. Outstanding assistance remains due when you change or lose employers and does not block another basic recovery job.

Finance shows the outstanding assistance balance and payroll repayments in the ledger.

## Loans

Loans can help when you need money, but they create weekly payments.

Before signing a loan, review:

- Total amount.
- Weekly payment.
- Total repayment.
- Interest.
- Your current balance.
- Your expected pay.
- Other weekly costs.

Missed loan payments can damage credit and reputation. Repeated missed payments can lead to default.

For reputation effects from missed payments and loan default, see [Career Reputation](/career-reputation).

Career credit starts at 700 and ranges from 300 to 850.

Credit can change when:

- A new loan is created.
- You make on-time payments.
- You fully pay off a loan.
- You miss a payment.
- A loan defaults.

The **Credit** strip shows your score on a 300 to 850 scale with a flag where each loan product unlocks. Locked offers still show the rate, term, and weekly payment you would get today, so you can see what you are working toward.

| Loan | Credit needed | Reputation needed | Career days | Term | Minimum APR |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bridge Credit | 520 | 5.0 | 30 | 78 weeks | 8% |
| Fleet Upgrade | 680 | 6.5 | 60 | 104 weeks | 14% |
| Career Ladder | 740 | 8.0 | 90 | 156 weeks | 18% |

Credit points:

| Event | Credit change |
| --- | ---: |
| On-time weekly payment | +2 |
| Loan fully paid off | +6 |
| Signing a new loan | -14, plus -8 for each loan you already hold |
| Missed payment | -38 |
| Default | -120 |

Standard loan rules include:

- You need at least 30 Career days before normal bank loans are available.
- The minimum credit score is 520.
- The bank will not sign a loan if your combined weekly loan payments would pass 35% of your weekly salary. When your current payments are already above that line, the Monthly budget marks the loan row.
- Standard users can hold 1 active standard loan.
- Stronger credit and reputation can allow 2 active standard loans.
- Loans use weekly payments over the loan term.
- Missed payments can add late fees.
- A loan can default after 4 missed weeks.

Loan default is serious. It damages credit heavily and can also reduce reputation.

### Paying a loan down early

Each loan you hold is drawn as a route from the day you signed it to the day you will be debt-free. Under it, **Pay extra** takes any amount you type, with quick buttons for common amounts. Before you pay, it shows how many weeks earlier you will finish and how much interest you will save. Extra money goes straight to principal: your weekly payment stays the same and the loan ends sooner. **Pay off** closes the loan when you have the full balance in cash. Both open a confirmation before any money moves.

## Property

Rental property turns savings into monthly income.

You pay the down payment and the bank lends the rest of the price. Tenants' rent covers the mortgage payment and upkeep, and what is left is added to your payday. Each property card shows what you pay now, what you keep every month, where that number comes from, how long until the down payment has come back, and how much of your living costs the rent covers.

| Property | Price | You pay now | Rent | Mortgage | Upkeep | You keep each month | Down payment back in | Most you can own |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Starter Duplex | $60,000 | $10,000 | $1,000 | $300 | $100 | $600 | about 17 weeks | 2 |
| Small Apartment Block | $200,000 | $35,000 | $3,600 | $1,000 | $325 | $2,275 | about 15 weeks | 1 |
| Urban Mixed-Use Complex | $600,000 | $90,000 | $9,000 | $2,100 | $600 | $6,300 | about 13 weeks | 1 |

Career time runs on real UTC days, so these are real weeks. Each tier has an ownership limit so rent stays a bonus on top of flying pay rather than replacing it.

If you are short of the down payment and a loan slot is free, the card offers the smallest loan that covers the gap. Make sure the down payment and any loan payment still leave room for medical checks, relocation, and normal living costs.

Property bought before the October 2026 rebalance keeps the monthly income it was bought with.

Property income is applied through the Career finance ledger. It belongs to Career Mode only.

## Personal events

Career Mode can occasionally apply random personal events.

These are not flight mistakes. They are life expenses designed to make money management matter.

Examples include:

- Unexpected medical bill.
- Car breakdown.
- Family emergency support.
- Home repair.
- Pet emergency vet bill.
- Tax adjustment notice.
- Urgent family travel.

When a personal event happens, it appears in the finance ledger and can be mentioned in the payroll summary.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the Ledger as a forecast. The Ledger is history.
- Taking loans without checking weekly payment pressure.
- Ignoring medical checks.
- Buying property while short on cash.
- Forgetting that relocation, leave, resignation, and penalties can affect money.
- Assuming payroll appears immediately after every flight. Payroll follows the Career payroll schedule.
